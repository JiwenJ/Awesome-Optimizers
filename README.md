| 2026-08-26 | [Muon with Finite Newton-Schulz: The Smoothing Benefit in Nonsmooth Nonconvex Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26288) | This analysis treats finite-depth Newton-Schulz as a beneficial smoothing of the discontinuous polar map rather than only an approximation error. Through a discounted online-to-nonconvex conversion, it proves that a depth growing logarithmically with target accuracy yields stationary-point guarantees for nonsmooth nonconvex objectives and leading sample-complexity terms matching the best known bounds; an intermediate-depth synthetic experiment is stable where exact-polar and deeper updates fluctuate. The guarantees use fixed operator-norm normalization, classical Taylor coefficients, gradients queried at random points between iterates, and bounded-oracle assumptions rather than practical Frobenius normalization, Muon's tuned quintic, or current-iterate gradients; experiments are limited to one synthetic 20-by-100 objective with no neural-network training or public code. |
| 2026-08-26 | [Spectral Allocation: Why Muon Outperforms Adam, and How to Improve Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25990) | SAMuon uses out-of-sample spectral probes to identify a volatile leading singular head and a more tolerant bulk, keeps the head at Muon's scale, and amplifies the bulk with either a log-rank-linear randomized-SVD profile or a rank-one power-iteration approximation in SAMuon-lite. Across 124M, 300M, and 1B modded-nanogpt models, both beat tuned AdamW and Scion Muon in every trained model/batch cell; SAMuon saves 13.3-24.0% of tokens to Muon's final loss and SAMuon-lite saves 13.3-22.1%, while adding 7.4% and 0.5% iteration time respectively on the 1B timing setup. The static profile is empirically transferred from smaller probes, ignores cross-direction and per-module interactions, theory assumes exact whitening, experiments stop at one GPT-style family and 1B parameters, and no public implementation is linked. |
| 2026-08-24 | [A Physical Response-and-Memory Model for Muon Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22994) [Code](https://github.com/orange4664/bimaxwell-track3-reproduction) | Bi-Maxwell interprets Muon's EMA momentum as internal stress and replaces its single relaxation time with a convex mixture of fast and slow states, leaving the polar response unchanged; the reported setting uses beta_f=0.85, beta_s=0.98, w=0.4385, and activates at step 1000. On a 124M modded-nanogpt benchmark, the bare tuned-Muon stack reaches the target at step 3210 instead of 3250, while the record stack reaches 2635 instead of 2690; an independent eight-seed H100 arm reaches 2645 and the full logs and scripts are public. Evidence is confined to one model and benchmark recipe, some reference arms differ in hardware or seed count, the schedule and mixture were selected by scans, and two FP32 memory states add about 680 MB at this scale. |
| 2026-08-21 | [RODE: A Radial-Orthogonal Decoupled Engine for Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21024) | RODE separates each managed matrix into its Frobenius radius and unit direction, gives the radius a scalar update, and applies Newton-Schulz-conditioned momentum in the directional tangent space through a geodesic step. Across GPT-2, a Qwen2-style 1.5B pretraining transfer, two image-classification tasks, and equally tuned Qwen3.5-9B full fine-tuning, it beats both original- and RMS-scaled Muon in every direct comparison; at 1.5B it lowers loss from 4.145 to 3.346 and final global norm from 11,964 to 2,183 versus Muon RMS. The study covers limited architectures and budgets, delegates nonmatrix parameters to AdamW, adds tangent/geodesic overhead, relies on idealized theory assumptions, and links no public code. |
| 2026-08-21 | [Scaling Muon for Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20818) | Periodic Row-wise Muon applies a full five-step Newton-Schulz spectral refresh every three steps and uses a gamma=0.15 RowNorm update on the intervening steps, with sharded RowNorm plus bucketed all-gather overlap. On 1.3B-15B MMDiTs trained for 60K steps on 256 H100s, vanilla Muon improves best generative quality over AdamW by 12.9-19.1%; the periodic variant stays within 0.5% of Muon at 1.3B-4B and improves it at 9B, while cutting optimizer time 46.9-54.3%, end-to-end step time 15.7-24.3%, and logical communication 66.7%. Results use one DiT family, dataset, resolution, topology, and fixed global K/gamma; refresh steps still materialize full momentum, and no public implementation is linked. |
| 2026-08-14 | [Approximate Muon with low-rank adapters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14492) | sMuon linearizes the full-weight Muon objective, projects that target into the tangent space expressible by a LoRA update through least squares, and reduces the spectral work to a matmul-only 2r-by-2r core while preserving first-order parameterization invariance. Across Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and Moonlight-16B-A3B SFT on 11 evaluations, it is strongest on the Muon-pretrained Moonlight model and leads six tasks; in ReLoRA pretraining it reaches 3.66 validation loss versus 3.83 for AdamW, 3.70 for per-factor Muon, and 3.66 for Riemannion while running about 30% faster than Riemannion. Gains are model- and evaluation-dependent, several 3B cells still favor AdamW, competing low-rank baselines are author reimplementations, and no public code is linked. |
| 2026-08-13 | [Federated Compositional Muon Optimizer for Matrix-Wise Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12710) | FedCoMuon combines compositional gradient tracking with Muon-style orthogonalized momentum for heterogeneous federated matrix models, while FedCoMuon-VR adds variance reduction and attains an O(epsilon^-3) sample-complexity guarantee for nonconvex objectives. Experiments cover a highly imbalanced ten-client MNIST CNN, an eight-layer 768-hidden Transformer on WikiText-2, and CIFAR-10 CNN/ViT-Tiny meta-learning, where the proposed methods report the best overall convergence or accuracy in several settings. The evidence remains small-scale and figure-based, with no large language model, systems-level communication or wall-clock benchmark, confidence intervals, or public implementation. |
| 2026-08-12 | [Dion3: Full-stack orthogonal updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11612) [Code](https://github.com/microsoft/dion) | Dion3 combines smaller-side Gram Newton-Schulz, symmetric CuteDSL kernels, row or column sub-selection, and shape megabatching; full selection preserves Muon or NorMuon while fractional selection defines a cheaper optimizer. On 1B models trained for 100B ClimbMix tokens, tuned fractional variants finish about 0.01 loss below tuned NorMuon; at 3B-14B over 10B tokens, one-quarter selection lowers loss by 0.011-0.027 and wins downstream accuracy at three of four scales. Kernels yield 1.5x dense and 2x MoE optimizer speedups and quarter selection another 3.7x, reducing a 7B four-GH200 Muon step from 26x to 4x AdamW cost. Results lack seed uncertainty and remain concentrated in the authors' language-model stack. |
| 2026-08-10 | [Second-Order Muon Done Right](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09763) | Go-MUON replaces Muon's unweighted polar geometry with positive-definite left and right maps and maps the conditioned matrix-sign solution back so the raw update exactly solves the matched weighted spectral oracle; its concrete K-FAC-root form reuses curvature factors across steps. Three-seed Tiny Shakespeare and Penn Treebank comparisons improve trajectory summaries by 3.71% and 0.38%, four-step refresh cuts mean step time about 20%, and one-seed modular-addition screens reach sustained 99% at 290 versus 2,320 updates and 220 versus 4,520 for Muon on two moduli. Evidence is small-scale, the largest effects come from one-seed grokking cases, the PTB gain is modest, and no public code is linked. |
| 2026-08-07 | [Post-Grokking Collapse at the Representation-Readout Interface in Muon-Trained Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07436) [Code](https://github.com/Na00s/muon-grokking) | Under the standard split that routes hidden matrices to Muon and embeddings plus the readout to AdamW, every one of nine modular-addition configurations groks faster and then loses generalization; across five paired seeds the Muon arm records 137-321 sub-threshold evaluations, while the selected AdamW arm also dips on four seeds but far less severely. After the training set is solved, the Muon group's applied step has near-zero elasticity to gradient magnitude and moves 8.0 times farther per parameter than the auxiliary group; freezing embeddings and readout eliminates all collapses over 451,400 post-grokking steps. Fourier interventions distinguish circuit failure from masking and restore one masked model to 99.9% by rescaling its task family, but the evidence remains a small modular-arithmetic transformer study rather than language pretraining. |
| 2026-08-06 | [Muon on the Stiefel Manifold Admits an Exact Closed-Form Update](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06218) | Skewon reformulates Muon's spectral-norm linear minimization oracle on the Stiefel manifold as an equivalent problem over a skew-symmetric matrix and derives an exact closed-form solution. A QR reduction evaluates the matrix sign on a 2p-by-2p core, giving O(np^2+p^3) work when p is much smaller than n, and the resulting method has an O(T^-1/2) first-order stationarity guarantee for smooth nonconvex objectives. Fifty-run CPU tests compare oracle time and error against iterative manifold-Muon alternatives, but they use random Gaussian matrices and exact SVDs rather than neural training or practical finite Newton-Schulz approximations. |
| 2026-08-05 | [MALT: Lightweight Curvature-Aware Muon via Diagonal Preconditioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05088) | MALT tracks row- and column-wise diagonal preconditioners, orthogonalizes the preconditioned momentum, maps the result back, and uses norm grafting; MALTER adds a scalar noise-adaptive stepsize. On tuned OpenWebText pretraining, MALT lowers Muon's validation loss from 3.0472/2.9719/2.7848 to 3.0358/2.9611/2.7723 on GPT-2 124M/355M/774M, while MALTER reaches 3.0231/2.9442/2.7684. At 355M, state rises only from 1553.38 to 1554.88 MB and step time from 2637.62 to 2652.55/2666.22 ms on one H100; evidence remains one model/data family without seed uncertainty, and the stated stochastic guarantee has weaker dimension dependence than existing Muon bounds. |
| 2026-08-05 | [On MUON optimization: From non-convergence to an error analysis with Polar Express and the Newton-Schulz polynomial from implementations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04607) | This analysis embeds the implementation polynomial and Polar Express in a generalized Muon family with arbitrary Newton-Schulz polynomial degree and iteration count. For a simple class of stochastic optimization problems it proves that, for almost every mini-batch size, Muon does not converge exactly to the solution as the number of gradient steps tends to infinity, then bounds the residual error jointly by the training horizon, batch size, and generalized sign approximation. The examples cover quadratic stochastic objectives and L2-regularized logistic regression; the 82-page treatment supplies no deep-network training, accelerator measurements, or empirical evidence that distinguishes practical PE and NS schedules. |
| 2026-08-04 | [Muon Meets Mamba: Spectral Optimization for State Space Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03941) | A controlled Mamba-2 130M study finds that Muon helps selectively: assigning it only to the homogeneous output projections is consistently better than assigning it to the heterogeneous fused input projection or to both. On OpenWebText, output-only Muon matches AdamW's one-billion-token loss after about 0.58B tokens and lowers the final loss by 0.116; the endpoint ordering persists across two corpora at 1B and 2.6B tokens and in a single-seed 50B-token run. Conditioning improves wherever Muon is applied, but the better-conditioned input projection is not the one that helps; most cells use only two seeds, and the 50B downstream average is essentially tied with AdamW. |
| 2026-08-04 | [Joint Affine Spectral Shaping: Coupling Weight and Bias Updates Beyond Weight-Only Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02991) | JRI appends scaled bias momentum to weight momentum as [M_W, alpha m_b], applies a capped regularized-inverse SVD, and keeps the reconstructed final column as the physical bias update. In five paired BERT-mini IMDb runs, weight-only inverse shaping raises exact-SVD Muon from 84.903% to 85.562% validation-selected test accuracy, while joint bias reaches 85.738%; a 22-seed run replicates JRI alone at 85.743%. The incremental bias gain is therefore small and confined to one model and task, about 98.6% of inverse gains hit the cap, exact thin SVD is roughly 50% slower than Newton-Schulz, and the promised permanent code/data repository is not yet public. |
| 2026-08-03 | [CMuon: Accelerating and Stabilizing Diffusion Transformer Training via Chunked Momentum Orthogonalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02502) | CMuon attributes Muon's late-stage Diffusion Transformer plateau to applying one polar factor to fused but functionally distinct AdaLN, QKV, and gate/up blocks; it splits those tensors before Newton-Schulz and rescales the stacked update. On ImageNet-1K, a 675M DiT reaches FID 1.18 in 200 epochs, ahead of AdamW's 1.21 at 400, while 130M ablations find the three chunk families complementary and batched or fused kernels bring measured throughput near AdamW. Evidence is confined to class-conditional DiTs, a narrow learning-rate sweep, and apparently single runs without uncertainty. |
| 2026-08-03 | [A Continuous-Time Analysis of Smoothed Matrix-Polar Spectral Gradient Flows for Muon-Type Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01911) | Replacing the nonsmooth polar map with h_epsilon(G)=G(G^T G+epsilon I)^(-1/2) gives globally Lipschitz spectral feedback and direct or momentum continuous-time Muon-type flows. The paper proves well-posedness, stationary-set convergence, nonconvex, convex, and PL rates, plus O(epsilon) finite-horizon agreement with ideal polar flows while relevant singular values stay away from zero; a quadratic-layer model yields a local advantage test combining gradient effective rank and gradient-activation covariance similarity. Its experiments are deterministic 2x2-12x12 toys, and the model deliberately omits finite steps, stochastic gradients, practical momentum recursions, Newton-Schulz error, and rank transitions. |
| 2026-07-31 | [Sign compression for Muon: SignMuon, MuonSign, and the Limits of Error Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29674) [Code](https://github.com/intsystems/SignMuon) | Every direct one-bit sign placement around Muon's linear minimization oracle can ascend even on simple objectives, and standard error feedback on the Muon output can fail for every smoothness constant, step size, and momentum. Applying EF21 to the gradient instead gives O(T^-1/2) nonconvex guarantees for MuonUSign and bidirectionally compressed MuonSign. Centralized and federated CIFAR-10 plus a single-run nanoGPT speedrun reverse the theory ranking: the theoretically unsafe sign-after-LMO heuristic is strongest, so the communication accounting is exact while neural evidence remains modest-scale. |
| 2026-07-28 | [Sharpness-Aware Minimization and Muon: Robustness under the Spectral Norm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26001) | SpecSAM-Muon replaces SAM's Euclidean inner perturbation with a layerwise spectral-norm perturbation for matrix blocks, then applies Muon to the outer gradient while routing nonmatrix parameters through conventional optimizers. In three-seed ImageNet-1K runs, ViT-S/16 and ResNet-50 reach 80.23% and 78.55% validation accuracy, beating matched SAM-Muon and Muon; the same perturbation is much less effective with AdamW or SGDW, indicating that inner and outer geometries interact. It requires two backward passes plus inner Newton-Schulz, evaluates only two vision models, uses nonexhaustive tuning, and reports some diagnostics from single runs. |
| 2026-07-26 | [Scale Weight Decay and Train Better](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23777) | Scale Weight Decay multiplies decoupled decay by the current-to-maximum learning-rate ratio, making shrinkage quadratic in the current learning rate and vanish as the schedule anneals. Theory shows that this preserves the unregularized asymptotic stationarity of SGD and Muon instead of retaining constant-decay bias, while parameter norms stabilize empirically. Across 72.7M-932.4M LLaMA-style mixture-of-experts models on FineWeb, Muon-SW reaches matched losses in 21.7-29.4% fewer steps than standard decay. Evidence is limited to one MoE family and data domain, with single matched runs and no per-scale hyperparameter search. |
| 2026-07-22 | [An Isotropy-Preserving Spectral Cap for Muon: Theory and Three Case Studies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19771) | Muon Spectral Cap estimates a data-coupled top singular direction by sample covariance and power iteration, then removes from each Muon step the first-order component that would increase the top mode; non-negative second-order spectral growth still lets the matrix rotate, switch top directions, and expand non-top modes. In three small case studies it preserves nanoGPT validation loss while improving FFN isotropy, prevents a 64-expert router's layer-0 rank-one collapse, and keeps an unpatched BF16 FlashAttention run stable through 120K steps where the uncapped baseline crashes. This is a preliminary report whose theory assumes exact scale invariance, with small and partly single-seed experiments and one logged FlashAttention counterfactual. |
| 2026-07-22 | [Geometry-Aware Optimizers as Scale Mixtures: Muon, Spectral Shrinkage, and Fast Compute](https://ssrn.com/abstract=7035060) | This scale-mixture view unifies geometry-aware optimizers as priors and maximum-a-posteriori updates: SGD is Euclidean, sign methods and Adam are coordinatewise, Muon is the operator-norm or polar endpoint, and Adafactor or Shampoo arise from hierarchical and factored scale mixtures. It connects Adafactor's maximum-likelihood scales with factored moments and Newton-Schulz computation, while rotated anisotropic toy objectives illustrate coordinate dependence versus spectral or Kronecker invariance. This is primarily a conceptual synthesis: it introduces no benchmarked optimizer and validates its claims on illustrative toy problems rather than language or vision training. |
| 2026-07-21 | [ISO: An RLVR-Native Optimization Stack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19331) [Code](https://github.com/zhuhanqing/ISO) | ISO parameterizes each matrix as U Sigma_0 V^T, fixes the base spectrum, applies AdamW or Muon to the two singular-frame variables, and polar-retracts them; its companion ISO-Merger composes shared-base RL experts without data or rollouts. On Qwen3-4B, ISO-Muon matches the strongest tuned Muon endpoint in 220 rather than 300 updates and finishes higher, while ISO-AdamW reaches Qwen3-8B AdamW's 270-update accuracy by update 100. FP64 SVD retraction adds about 7% end-to-end RL step time and increases nominal trainable/state storage; evidence is limited to 1.5B-8B RLVR, and the repository currently releases the merger while ISO-Optimizer code is still promised. |
| 2026-07-20 | [PoLoRA: A Preconditioned Orthogonalized LoRA Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17620) [Code](https://github.com/nikhilgsh/polora) | PoLoRA makes Muon-style spectral descent product-aware for LoRA, preconditions factor updates with a diagonal Kronecker approximation derived from per-sample loss control, and rescales them to bound both factor and merged-weight steps. Across 1B-8B code, math, and Bengali instruction tuning, it reaches tuned Adam's final held-out loss in 1.2-1.7 times fewer steps with at most 3% per-step overhead, while ablations split the gain between curvature and magnitude control. Its derivation linearizes both loss and product updates and uses approximate diagonal curvature; evaluation centers on held-out loss rather than broad downstream quality or long training horizons. |
| 2026-07-17 | [When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16169) [Code](https://github.com/x66ccff/verl-muon) | The expanded study maps fan-in Muon across GRPO, GiGPO, and GraphGPO on ALFWorld with Qwen2.5 models from 0.5B to 3B and mostly five-seed matched cells. High-rate Muon improves pooled late success under a shared KL/clipping recipe, but the lower heuristic-matched rate is inconsistent and tuned AdamW nearly matches its 3B GraphGPO AUC. Standard Muon applies 3.53 times AdamW's hidden-matrix update RMS; a full-budget RMS-matched control removes the gain, tying the result to Muon's scaling convention rather than proving universal spectral superiority. Applied updates are much flatter spectrally, and a WebShop transfer is favorable, but conclusions remain recipe- and environment-dependent. |
| 2026-07-16 | [Muse: Representation Geometry of Muon Beyond Normalized Momentum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14536) | Muse makes a hidden design choice in Muon explicit: the Frobenius-isometric matrix representation supplied to the polar map. It unifies native, nearest-square, skinny, and vector views, proves short-side-dependent spectral-descent and stochastic stationarity bounds, and links early dissipation to a Marchenko–Pastur nuclear/Frobenius shape factor. In three-seed LLaMA2-130M/600M runs, balanced reshaping nearly matches native Muon (within 0.003 validation loss at 600M), while shrinking the short side approaches normalized momentum. |
| 2026-07-14 | [Reassessing Muon for Matrix Factorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13246) | Through equal learning-rate sweeps across low-rank factorization, matrix completion, nonnegative matrix factorization, tensor-train recovery, conditioning, and spectrum shapes, this study finds no universal Muon advantage. Tuned AdamW or GD beat Muon by orders of magnitude on standard factorization, whereas Muon remains strongest on NMF and some severely ill-conditioned completion cases. Optimizer-optimal learning rates differ by 1.5–3.1 decades, making default-rate comparisons unreliable; evidence is limited to controlled, small synthetic recovery problems. |
| 2026-07-13 | [SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20548) [Code](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Emerging-Optimizers) | This large-scale study compares AdamW, Muon, MOP, SOAP, and KL-SOAP on dense and mixture-of-experts language models up to 72B-A8B over 1T-3T tokens, matching update RMS for fairer tuning. Muon and SOAP outperform AdamW, while KL-SOAP slightly leads Muon and MOP when memory is available; per-step QR and KL-Shampoo stabilize SOAP at very large batches. An exact owner-based layerwise distribution overlaps all-gather with computation, and operator tests find Muon can destabilize Mamba2 Conv1D, motivating selective AdamW fallback. Epsilon and batch-ramp tuning are incomplete, and SOAP support for tensor parallelism and fused QKV remains limited. |
| 2026-07-07 | [MatrixFSDP: communication-free matrix optimizers under ZeRO-3 parameter sharding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05895) | MatrixFSDP changes ZeRO-3 placement rather than approximating Muon: each 2D weight has one owner rank, so normal backward reduction delivers its complete gradient for a local Newton-Schulz step, while balanced planning, segment-wise P2P collectives, and checkpoint resharding preserve FSDP semantics. On 64 A100s it matched full-matrix Muon with ZeRO-3 memory and cut eight-node optimizer-step latency by 54.6x, producing up to 2.15x end-to-end speedup. |
| 2026-07-06 | [The Active Ingredient in Muon's Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20512) | Across 258 modular-arithmetic runs with a one-layer four-head transformer, orthogonalization alone matches full Muon's grokking behavior, whereas Muon's post-orthogonalization spectral scaling can be removed. Orthogonalizing optimizers converge to lower-spectral-norm, more distributed Fourier solutions; one Newton-Schulz step crosses the accuracy threshold sooner but collapses in 6 of 8 runs, while five steps grok more reliably. The evidence is confined to one small architecture and task family, and first-crossing metrics can reverse stability conclusions. The paper declares a code repository, but that link was not publicly accessible when audited on 2026-07-30. |
| 2026-07-01 | [Muon as a Residual Connection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01124) [Code](https://github.com/huanghao-sss/muon_interpretation) | The paper offers a mechanistic, not architectural, analogy between Muon's orthogonalized update and a residual connection: weaker immediate gradient alignment can preserve representations that downstream layers can use more easily. In controlled two-layer linear models, SGD fitted the local factor faster, but Muon completed end-to-end learning in fewer total steps under every tested joint or alternating schedule because the downstream factor adapted faster; the evidence remains limited to linear settings. |
| 2026-06-29 | [Muon learns balanced solutions in matrix factorization without slow saddle-to-saddle dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30509) | For exact-polar, momentum-free Muon on matrix factorization, the analysis shows that early alignment is followed by nearly uniform additive growth of singular values, so smaller target modes finish first instead of traversing gradient descent's condition-number-limited saddle staircase. It identifies a Muon-specific conserved square-root Gram imbalance, proves balanced solutions from vanishing initialization, and validates alignment scaling and a two-stage learning-rate schedule, while noting that large fixed steps oscillate near the optimum. |
| 2026-06-29 | [MuonSSM: Orthogonalizing State Space Models for Sequence Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30461) [Code](https://github.com/t-khanusa/MuonSSM/tree/main) | MuonSSM moves Muon's orthogonalization idea from parameter optimization into recurrent memory, combining a momentum state with one-step Newton-Schulz-normalized low-rank writes while retaining a block-affine recurrence that supports associative scans. Theory supplies an additional gradient path, bounded singular amplification, and rank enrichment, and controlled 170M-token-model, vision, and time-series comparisons report stronger long-context retrieval and robustness; ablations attribute higher momentum rank and validation accuracy to Newton-Schulz rather than simple Frobenius normalization. |
| 2026-06-28 | [Dead-Direction Conditioners: Gauge-Equivariant Preconditioning for Deep Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29176) | Dead-Direction Conditioners precondition only the horizontal, function-changing component of an update while reducing each vertical gauge-orbit direction to a scalar, covering common shift, rescaling, normalization, and attention-rotation symmetries. The construction is exactly gauge-equivariant for Adam and composes with a compatible Muon orthogonalizer; experiments found DDCMuon grokked 10 of 11 deep-model seeds where plain Muon grokked none, with additional language-model and vision gains. |
| 2026-06-26 | [Aurora: A Leverage-Aware Spectral Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27715) [Code](https://github.com/tilde-research/aurora-release) | Aurora traces neuron death in tall MLP matrices to persistently unequal row leverage in Muon's polar update, then replaces post-hoc row normalization with an iterative projection toward both semi-orthogonality and equal row norms. Across 340M and 1.1B models trained for 100B tokens, it prevented the diagnosed dead-neuron feedback and improved aggregate benchmarks over Muon, including a reported 9.1-point MMLU gain at 1.1B, with modest distributed overhead. |
| 2026-06-25 | [Hierarchical Muon: Tiled Newton-Schulz Updates for Efficient Muon Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27216) [Code](https://github.com/tang0389/himuon) | Hierarchical Muon applies the finite Newton-Schulz map independently to fixed-size matrix tiles, yielding a distinct locally coupled optimizer rather than an approximation that converges to full Muon as iterations increase. Its cost scales with tile size instead of the smaller full matrix dimension and exposes small batched GEMMs; diagnostics show larger tiles retain more global behavior, while single-seed Qwen3 experiments kept loss close to full Muon with lower optimizer and wall-clock time. |
