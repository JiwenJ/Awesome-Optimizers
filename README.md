| 2026-07-16 | [Muse: Representation Geometry of Muon Beyond Normalized Momentum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14536) | Muse makes a hidden design choice in Muon explicit: the Frobenius-isometric matrix representation supplied to the polar map. It unifies native, nearest-square, skinny, and vector views, proves short-side-dependent spectral-descent and stochastic stationarity bounds, and links early dissipation to a Marchenko–Pastur nuclear/Frobenius shape factor. In three-seed LLaMA2-130M/600M runs, balanced reshaping nearly matches native Muon (within 0.003 validation loss at 600M), while shrinking the short side approaches normalized momentum. |
| 2026-07-14 | [Reassessing Muon for Matrix Factorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13246) | Through equal learning-rate sweeps across low-rank factorization, matrix completion, nonnegative matrix factorization, tensor-train recovery, conditioning, and spectrum shapes, this study finds no universal Muon advantage. Tuned AdamW or GD beat Muon by orders of magnitude on standard factorization, whereas Muon remains strongest on NMF and some severely ill-conditioned completion cases. Optimizer-optimal learning rates differ by 1.5–3.1 decades, making default-rate comparisons unreliable; evidence is limited to controlled, small synthetic recovery problems. |
| 2026-07-07 | [MatrixFSDP: communication-free matrix optimizers under ZeRO-3 parameter sharding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05895) | MatrixFSDP changes ZeRO-3 placement rather than approximating Muon: each 2D weight has one owner rank, so normal backward reduction delivers its complete gradient for a local Newton-Schulz step, while balanced planning, segment-wise P2P collectives, and checkpoint resharding preserve FSDP semantics. On 64 A100s it matched full-matrix Muon with ZeRO-3 memory and cut eight-node optimizer-step latency by 54.6x, producing up to 2.15x end-to-end speedup. |
| 2026-07-01 | [Muon as a Residual Connection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01124) [Code](https://github.com/huanghao-sss/muon_interpretation) | The paper offers a mechanistic, not architectural, analogy between Muon's orthogonalized update and a residual connection: weaker immediate gradient alignment can preserve representations that downstream layers can use more easily. In controlled two-layer linear models, SGD fitted the local factor faster, but Muon completed end-to-end learning in fewer total steps under every tested joint or alternating schedule because the downstream factor adapted faster; the evidence remains limited to linear settings. |
| 2026-06-29 | [Muon learns balanced solutions in matrix factorization without slow saddle-to-saddle dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30509) | For exact-polar, momentum-free Muon on matrix factorization, the analysis shows that early alignment is followed by nearly uniform additive growth of singular values, so smaller target modes finish first instead of traversing gradient descent's condition-number-limited saddle staircase. It identifies a Muon-specific conserved square-root Gram imbalance, proves balanced solutions from vanishing initialization, and validates alignment scaling and a two-stage learning-rate schedule, while noting that large fixed steps oscillate near the optimum. |
| 2026-06-29 | [MuonSSM: Orthogonalizing State Space Models for Sequence Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30461) [Code](https://github.com/t-khanusa/MuonSSM/tree/main) | MuonSSM moves Muon's orthogonalization idea from parameter optimization into recurrent memory, combining a momentum state with one-step Newton-Schulz-normalized low-rank writes while retaining a block-affine recurrence that supports associative scans. Theory supplies an additional gradient path, bounded singular amplification, and rank enrichment, and controlled 170M-token-model, vision, and time-series comparisons report stronger long-context retrieval and robustness; ablations attribute higher momentum rank and validation accuracy to Newton-Schulz rather than simple Frobenius normalization. |
| 2026-06-28 | [Dead-Direction Conditioners: Gauge-Equivariant Preconditioning for Deep Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29176) | Dead-Direction Conditioners precondition only the horizontal, function-changing component of an update while reducing each vertical gauge-orbit direction to a scalar, covering common shift, rescaling, normalization, and attention-rotation symmetries. The construction is exactly gauge-equivariant for Adam and composes with a compatible Muon orthogonalizer; experiments found DDCMuon grokked 10 of 11 deep-model seeds where plain Muon grokked none, with additional language-model and vision gains. |
| 2026-06-26 | [Aurora: A Leverage-Aware Spectral Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27715) [Code](https://github.com/tilde-research/aurora-release) | Aurora traces neuron death in tall MLP matrices to persistently unequal row leverage in Muon's polar update, then replaces post-hoc row normalization with an iterative projection toward both semi-orthogonality and equal row norms. Across 340M and 1.1B models trained for 100B tokens, it prevented the diagnosed dead-neuron feedback and improved aggregate benchmarks over Muon, including a reported 9.1-point MMLU gain at 1.1B, with modest distributed overhead. |
| 2026-06-25 | [Hierarchical Muon: Tiled Newton-Schulz Updates for Efficient Muon Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27216) [Code](https://github.com/tang0389/himuon) | Hierarchical Muon applies the finite Newton-Schulz map independently to fixed-size matrix tiles, yielding a distinct locally coupled optimizer rather than an approximation that converges to full Muon as iterations increase. Its cost scales with tile size instead of the smaller full matrix dimension and exposes small batched GEMMs; diagnostics show larger tiles retain more global behavior, while single-seed Qwen3 experiments kept loss close to full Muon with lower optimizer and wall-clock time. |
| 2026-06-25 | [DMuon: Efficient Distributed Muon Training with Near-Adam Overhead](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27153) [Code](https://github.com/X-Square-Robot/dmuon) | DMuon preserves exact Muon while assigning each matrix and its optimizer state to one rank, computing Newton-Schulz once, and hiding hierarchical reduction and publication behind training through lookahead overlap. Batched Gram-form orthogonalization, symmetry-aware kernels, autotuning, and measured-cost owner placement produced 1.48-3.01x end-to-end and 6.85-163x optimizer-step speedups on language and embodied-foundation workloads, bringing average end-to-end overhead to within 2% of AdamW without framework changes. |
| 2026-06-24 | [Tensorion: A Tensor-Aware Generalization of the Muon Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25975) | Tensorion generalizes Muon's matrix spectral steepest-descent view to higher-order tensors by introducing a tractable dual norm that upper-bounds the NP-hard tensor spectral norm; its linear minimization oracle selects an unfolding by nuclear norm and applies a matrix polar update, exactly recovering Muon at order two. An offline unfolding heuristic avoids repeated selection, and ResNet, Swin, and ViT experiments improved accuracy over SGD and Adam, though evaluation is confined to modest vision benchmarks. |
| 2026-06-22 | [Muown Implicitly Performs Angular Step-size Decay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23637) [Code](https://github.com/fhueb/angular-muown) | The analysis interprets Muown as Riemannian descent on a row-oblique manifold: growth of the unused ambient row norm acts as a gauge variable that implicitly decays angular steps at roughly inverse square-root time. AngularMuown instead fixes unit directions and explicitly schedules a width-transferable angular multiplier independently of Adam's radial scale; FineWeb-Edu dense and mixture-of-experts experiments lowered loss and showed more transferable learning rates, alongside a nonconvex stationarity guarantee. |
| 2026-06-22 | [Non-Euclidean SGD for Structured Optimization: Unified Analysis and Improved Rates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11466) | This work unifies normalized SGD, SignSGD or Lion, and Muon as momentum trust-region methods over operator subspaces and norms, analyzing them with structured smoothness and noise matrices rather than generic Euclidean conversions. The resulting theory explains when Muon benefits from high-rank solutions but low-rank curvature or noise, and shows extrapolation and momentum variance reduction can match one-sided Shampoo or AdaGrad rates in convex settings; it is a theoretical contribution without empirical validation. |
| 2026-06-19 | [Convergence Analysis of Muon-type Methods with Inexact LMO in the Degenerate Case](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21581) | The paper removes a fragile assumption in prior inexact-linear-oracle analyses of Muon-type methods: the smallest positive singular value of the rescaled momentum need not remain bounded away from zero. By replacing proximity to a nonunique polar factor with objective-alignment and update-size conditions, and treating low-momentum layers separately, it derives stochastic nonconvex, deterministic PL, and star-convex weight-decay convergence under layer-wise generalized smoothness, with explicit dependence on solver thresholds, precision, and iteration count. |
| 2026-06-19 | [Towards Understanding the Power and Limits of the Muon Optimizer: A River-Valley Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21514) | In a mixed-spiked matrix-sensing model, the paper casts optimization as following an informative river through nuisance curvature and shows why Muon's scale-free spectral step travels quickly early but overshoots and oscillates near the river bottom after discarding residual magnitude. Extensions to nonconvex spectral rivers plus MNIST and controlled 250M-token language-model runs support switching from Muon to AdamW or gradient descent for late refinement, which improves stability and final loss without claiming universal superiority. |
| 2026-06-16 | [MGUP: A Momentum-Gradient Alignment Update Policy for Stochastic Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17526) [Code](https://github.com/MaeChd/MGUP) | MGUP ranks coordinates by update-gradient alignment, amplifies a fixed top fraction, and gives the remainder a smaller nonzero step, unlike cautious masking schemes that discard misaligned coordinates; the rule plugs into AdamW, Lion, and Muon. It improves the underlying optimizers across masked-autoencoder training, language-model pretraining, and GLUE or GSM8K fine-tuning, while its near-optimal stochastic nonconvex convergence proof currently covers only AdamW without weight decay, leaving Lion and Muon theory open. |
| 2026-06-15 | [Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them II: Hyperball Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899) | Hyperball optimization fixes every Transformer matrix at its initialization Frobenius radius, normalizes the base optimizer's proposed direction, and projects each step back to the sphere, directly controlling angular learning instead of relying on weight-decay equilibrium. In Qwen3-style models up to 1.2B parameters, MuonH delivered a reported 20-30% token-equivalent gain over weight-decayed Muon and substantially reduced optimal-learning-rate drift across scale; scale-absorbing normalization layers motivate its expressivity argument. |
| 2026-06-15 | [CacheMuon: Using Temporal Preconditioning To Approximate Polar Factor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16371) | CacheMuon reuses the left preconditioner obtained from a stabilized Gram-form Newton-Schulz solve on recent momentum, applying it cheaply to the current matrix until an orthogonality residual triggers refresh. Its inexact-Muon analysis separates fresh-solver error from Lipschitz-controlled Gram staleness and gives convergence when their aggregate remains below one; GPT-2 and ResNet experiments show conservative thresholds match fresh Muon while cutting orthogonalization FLOPs, with aggressive caching exposing a clear savings-quality tradeoff. |
| 2026-06-12 | [Free Heavy-Tailed Lunch for Muon: A Theoretical Justification of Empirical Success](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14560) | This paper analyzes Muon under genuinely heavy-tailed gradient noise with only a finite p-th moment, using martingale inequalities in the optimizer's native Banach geometry instead of dimension-costly Euclidean norm conversions. It proves optimal normalized steepest-descent rates for uniformly smooth duals and matching lower bounds, with improved dimension dependence for nuclear-norm Muon; Pythia-70M measurements find native noise ratios milder than worst case and gains from Schatten geometry up to an intermediate order before plateauing. |
| 2026-06-11 | [Muon$^p$: Muon with Fractional Spectral Powers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13867) [Code](https://github.com/princeton-pli/muon-p) | Muon^p interpolates between Muon and gradient descent by replacing each singular value with its fractional power and identifies the update as steepest descent under a corresponding Schatten norm. After proving fixed univariate polynomial iterations cannot realize noninteger powers, it derives SVD-free odd bivariate matrix recurrences; Llama 1B and 3B fine-tuning experiments outperform Muon on math and code tasks at competitive runtime, while mixed pretraining results suggest the best power is regime dependent. |
| 2026-06-11 | [LoRA-Muon: Spectral Steepest Descent on the Low-Rank Manifold](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12921) | LoRA-Muon solves a spectral linear minimization problem on the tangent space of the fixed-rank matrix manifold rather than optimizing LoRA factors independently, using Gram-whitened factor gradients and matrix-sign updates with a split trust region. The induced composed-matrix update is invariant to arbitrary invertible LoRA reparameterizations; compute-matched six-seed TinyShakespeare tests show rank-2 behavior near dense training and lower mean validation loss at rank 32, but evidence remains deliberately small-scale. |
| 2026-06-09 | [FOGO: Forgetting-aware Orthogonalization Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10406) | FOGO treats minibatch and continual-learning forgetting as gradient interference, then fuses slow and fast orthogonalized momentum streams, compresses conflicts with a random projection and spherical centroid memory, and lifts corrections through a proximal solve without retaining raw examples. Across imbalanced and continual vision, LLaVA-7B continual VQA, and 49.2B-token GPT-2 training it improved retention; language pretraining converged faster early but reached a similar final loss, clarifying that its main advantage is forgetting control. |
| 2026-06-08 | [Muon Learns More Robust and Transferable Features than Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09658) | Rather than explaining Muon only through faster optimization, this work links its orthogonalized updates to representation quality: layerwise probes show larger logit margins, while hidden-state spectra have higher effective rank. Matched-budget ImageNet and FineWeb pretraining finds better corruption robustness and downstream transfer than Adam or SGD, and a stylized multi-component classifier proves Muon’s spectral normalization yields both larger margins and effective rank at matched loss. |
| 2026-06-07 | [OptMuon: Closed-Loop Orthogonalized Momentum Methods for Stochastic Optimization with Zero-Noise Optimality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08783) | OptMuon couples Muon’s polar-factor momentum direction with a lagged, running-maximum-corrected AdaGrad-Norm coefficient, making step magnitude a closed-loop function of observed gradient and momentum energy rather than smoothness or noise constants. Its two variants prove noise-adaptive stationarity rates under average and individual smoothness and automatically recover a nearly optimal deterministic first-order rate when gradient noise vanishes, without hyperparameter retuning. |
| 2026-06-07 | [The Spectral Dynamics and Noise Geometry of Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08388) | This paper characterizes Muon as a flat-update-spectrum method rather than simple normalization or a nuclear-norm low-rank bias. Under projected polar flow it derives exact singular-value dynamics, an entropy-maximizing one-step result, a geometry-dependent flattening criterion, and polar-map noise sensitivity; matrix-sensing ablations separate these effects from norm-matched gradient descent, while NanoGPT and ViT controls reveal rank preservation and a regime-dependent performance reversal. |
| 2026-06-03 | [Why Muon Outperforms Adam: A Curvature Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04662) | Using second-order Taylor diagnostics at matched validation loss, the paper finds Muon and Adam obtain similar first-order gains and update norms, but Muon pays a much smaller Hessian penalty because its normalized directional sharpness is lower, with an average Adam-to-Muon ratio of 1.76. Zipf-PCFG experiments show imbalance widens this gap, layer decomposition locates it mainly within layers late in training, and heterogeneous quadratic models prove the balancing mechanism. |
| 2026-06-02 | [Spectral Scaling Laws of Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04058) | The paper tracks Muon momentum-buffer singular-value quantiles across GPT-2 models from 77M to 2.8B parameters and finds post-burn-in stabilization values obey layer-specific power laws. Rank-truncation ablations show roughly half the spectral directions must be orthogonalized for full-Muon quality; extrapolation predicts standard five-step Newton-Schulz remains sufficient through mid-late layers, whereas final projections with exponents as steep as -0.96 require a more accurate, layer-aware configuration at frontier scale. |
| 2026-06-02 | [Denoise First, Orthogonalize Later: Understanding Momentum in Muon via Spectral Filtering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03899) [Code](https://github.com/yinleung/Denoise-Ortho) | The work explains Muon’s ordering—momentum before polar orthogonalization—as spectral denoising: under a coherent signal plus bounded-variance orthogonal perturbation, momentum suppresses tail singular values, widens the signal-to-noise gap, and stabilizes singular subspaces. It proves pre-polar updates align better with signal than post-polar or no-momentum alternatives, and synthetic, CIFAR-10, NanoGPT probes plus 350M LLaMA training confirm the predicted ordering and loss advantage. |
| 2026-06-01 | [A Note on Stability for Orthogonalized Matrix Momentum with Client Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01720) | This stability analysis extends Muon generalization theory to partially participating federated clients with heterogeneous sample sizes and fixed aggregation weights. A coupled-neighbor recursion retains each client’s exposure amplification and yields conditional high-probability risk-gap bounds; regularized polar and finite-step Newton-Schulz maps satisfy the needed Lipschitz condition, while exact matrix sign requires a shared spectral gap or Gaussian smoothing, a necessity backed by a scalar counterexample. |
| 2026-05-29 | [How Much Orthogonalization Does Muon Need?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00371) | To cut Muon’s orthogonalization cost, the paper derives a five-step cubic Newton-Schulz schedule targeting a BF16 singular-value band of [0.7, 1.3], using 10 dominant matrix multiplications versus 15 for five quintic steps. GPT-2 Small places cubic5, Polar Express, Muon-Jordan, and exact FP32 SVD within 0.003 validation loss, while 1B–4B hybrid MoE/Mamba runs keep cubic5 within about 0.001 of Muon-Jordan, showing training quality is not monotonic in polar accuracy. |
| 2026-05-29 | [Softsign: Smooth Sign in Your Optimizer For Better Parameter Heterogeneity Handling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31371) | SoftMuon relaxes Muon’s hard spectral sign with a smooth algebraic soft-sign map and a quantile-driven temperature that gradually shifts small singular directions from full-magnitude Muon steps to magnitude-sensitive updates. The Fenchel-regularized framework supplies stochastic nonconvex convergence guarantees; across character modeling, GraphLand, and 130M–720M language-model pretraining, SoftMuon improves the hard baseline, reaching 16.216 versus Muon’s 16.362 perplexity at 720M. |
| 2026-05-29 | [A Quadratic Lens on Muon: Orthogonalization, Invariance, and Implicit Preconditioning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=TVTwgZp0qe) | Using exact line search on matrix quadratics, this paper isolates PolarGD’s direction geometry and identifies a gain–curvature tradeoff: its exact one-step regime is row-isotropic rather than conditioned by Hessian isotropy, and unlike SignGD it is equivariant to orthogonal basis changes, as Zipf-regression rotations demonstrate. Beyond one step, PolarGD implicitly whitens iterate covariance; active-subspace analysis handles rank deficiency, while finite Newton–Schulz acts as a bounded filter that suppresses unstable tiny-singular-value amplification. |
| 2026-05-29 | [How Does Orthogonalization Adapt to the Neural-Network Hessian Structure? A Gradient Self Outer-Product Analysis at Initialization](https://openreview.net/forum?id=U812abpXRD) | Writing Muon’s implicit metric as (VVᵀ)¹ᐟ² ⊗ I, the paper connects orthogonalization to neural Hessians’ row-block diagonal dominance. Closed-form Gaussian-initialization calculations for symmetric matrix factorization, deep linear networks, and two-layer ReLU networks show E[GGᵀ] becomes diagonally dominant with width, so orthogonalization asymptotically reduces to rowwise L2 normalization and aligns its preconditioner with the network’s curvature structure. |
| 2026-05-29 | [Practical Muon Accelerates Projected Feature Learning in Scaling-Law Models](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ufLJLAUCO6) | In the finite-width projected two-layer student–teacher model, this work replaces projected SGD’s rank-one feature update with practical NS5 Muon and a tractable partial-polar population proxy. The proxy removes the small projected-gradient-norm bottleneck and predicts late-time acceleration; fixed-compute sweeps show positive width-scaling exponents for Muon versus negative ones for SGD on hard source-condition tasks, with readout-Hessian spectra indicating stronger low-rank feature alignment. |
| 2026-05-29 | [On How Muon Reshapes Skill Learning Dynamics](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WUsrhWHhU3) | Connecting Muon to neural scaling laws, the paper uses multi-task sparse parity to show spectral updates acquire rare and common skills more concurrently than Adam or normalized momentum descent, quantified by a new Skill Acquisition Lag metric and shorter coverage times. In a linear-attention in-context regression model, Muon decouples covariance eigenmodes and converges uniformly despite task- and input-frequency imbalance, explaining how parallel acquisition can improve scaling exponents. |
| 2026-05-29 | [Spectral Equalization Minimizes Total Training Energy: A Control-Theoretic Account of Muon's Advantage](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Nu8P86ml5b) | Treating optimizers as feedback controllers, the paper defines total training energy—the cumulative squared back-propagated signal—and proves that, on Kronecker-quadratic losses, it is a closed-loop H2 norm decomposing over Hessian modes. Convexity shows uniform contraction rates minimize this energy, linking Muon’s polar equalization to the optimum; MNIST MLPs find Muon has the lowest per-mode-energy Gini throughout and the lowest cumulative energy in two of three depths, mainly through low-curvature modes. |
| 2026-05-29 | [Signal Frequency Imbalance and Ill-Conditioning](https://openreview.net/forum?id=90AnFSLGXd) | Rather than attributing optimizer difficulty solely to curvature, this paper models signal-frequency imbalance as correlations between parameter directions and data subsets, producing effective low-rank gradient clusters. Language-model inner-layer measurements find semantically coherent clusters, while a simplified analysis shows minibatch averaging suppresses rare directions and creates ill-conditioning only at large batch sizes; the resulting regime dependence explains why Adam and Muon outperform SGD, and why Muon can surpass Adam in inner layers. |
| 2026-05-26 | [Can Entry-Wise Clipping Give Spectral Control of Stochastic Gradients?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27733) | Motivated by localized, entry-wise heavy-tailed gradient noise, the paper derives Bayes-inspired smooth shrinkage as a cheap spectral-control surrogate and applies it after Adam updates or before Muon orthogonalization. A localization metric and Cauchy-contamination analysis give standard fourth-order stationarity complexity; on 276M Modded-NanoGPT, shrinkage saves 7.6% of Adam’s tokens and 1.9% beyond Muon while restoring spike-corrupted spectral subspaces. |
| 2026-05-26 | [Convergence of Spectral Descent for Non-smooth Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26977) | Spectral Descent and its rank-truncated variant receive the first global linear convergence guarantees for convex, Lipschitz, sharp but nonsmooth objectives, with TSD interpreted as Ky Fan-norm steepest descent and able to reduce SVD cost. Adding decoupled weight decay turns the method into a conditional-subgradient Frank-Wolfe update with sublinear convergence; ReLU-network, linear-program, matrix-classification, and robust LAD low-rank-recovery experiments support the predicted acceleration and mixed-noise robustness. |
| 2026-05-26 | [When Muon Optimizer Meets Adversarial Training: A Theoretical and Empirical Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26929) | The paper adapts Muon to multi-norm adversarial training and proves its polar update imposes an operator-norm ceiling plus a nuclear-norm descent guarantee, limiting abrupt spectral growth without claiming small final weight norms. Across five CNN and ViT architectures and three threat norms, Muon strongly outperforms unstable AdamW and often matches SGD on CIFAR-10, but SGD remains better in the ImageNet pilot and SAM is usually stronger at extra backpropagation cost. |
| 2026-05-26 | [MONA: Muon Optimizer with Nesterov Acceleration for Scalable Language Model Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26842) | MONA inserts an exponential moving average of consecutive gradient differences before Muon’s momentum and Newton-Schulz stages, using this Hessian proxy to bias orthogonalized updates away from sharp directions while retaining spectral normalization. It proves nonconvex stationarity and sharp-minimum selectivity; 1B, 6B, and 68B MoE pretraining shows lower validation loss, with the 68B model improving average general capability from Muon’s 0.4478 to 0.4557, while MONA-Lite cuts added memory about 75%. |
| 2026-05-26 | [MuCon: Clipped Muon Updates for LLM Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26459) | MuCon replaces Muon’s polar factor with the Frobenius projection onto a spectral-norm ball, clipping only singular values above a threshold and therefore preserving moderate update geometry. Rather than presenting training results, the report derives exact low-rank-correction, polar/absolute-value, and rational-Newton formulations: it recommends partial SVD or Lanczos when few values violate the threshold and shows global matrix-function filters become ill-conditioned near the boundary unless their solves are explicitly regularized. |
| 2026-05-25 | [EMA-Nesterov: Stabilizing Nesterov's Lookahead for Accelerated Deep Learning Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25395) | EMA-Nesterov replaces Nesterov’s noisy one-step displacement lookahead with an exponential moving average of realized parameter updates, providing a low-pass trajectory estimate that wraps arbitrary base optimizers while retaining accelerated convex rates. Across NanoGPT/Llama pretraining with Adam, SOAP, Muon, and NorMuon, it consistently improves optimization; with tuned Muon it reaches validation loss 3.28 in roughly 6% fewer steps and remains effective across model and token scales. |
| 2026-05-23 | [Muon in Vision Transformers: Optimizer-Recipe Interactions and Gradient Spectra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24770) | This study shows that Muon’s advantage in Vision Transformers depends strongly on the training recipe rather than optimizer choice alone: augmentation, Mixup/CutMix, and label smoothing prevent late gradient-mode collapse, while Muon redistributes spectral energy more broadly than AdamW. Across ImageNet-100, Pl@ntNet-300K, segmentation, and masked-autoencoder pretraining, matched-recipe Muon consistently wins, including 37.7% versus 16.3% macro top-1 on Pl@ntNet under the full recipe. |
| 2026-05-22 | [Move on Muon : A Hamiltonian probability gradient flow perspective of Muon optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23871) | Rather than treating Muon orthogonalization as an ad hoc matrix operation, this paper derives a smooth regularized polar map as the gradient of a Fenchel-dual smoothing of the nuclear norm, yielding a mirror/proximal momentum interpretation. Its finite-particle and mean-field Hamiltonian formulations establish well-posedness, propagation of chaos, exact energy dissipation, and exponential objective convergence under stated geometry assumptions, then extend the construction to product-matrix features relevant to transformer mixtures of experts. |
| 2026-05-21 | [AMUSE: Anytime Muon with Stable Gradient Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22432) [Code](https://github.com/kjeiun/amuse) | AMUSE combines Muon’s bulk-oriented orthogonalized updates with Schedule-Free averaging, but replaces the latter’s fixed interpolation with a horizon-independent coefficient that moves gradient evaluation from the fast iterate toward the stable averaged iterate after warmup. River-valley measurements show this suppresses high-curvature wall oscillations without sacrificing early progress; on vision, segmentation, and FineWeb LLM pretraining it dominates AdamW, Muon, and schedule-free baselines, reaching Muon’s final 720M-model result in 1.51× fewer steps. |
| 2026-05-21 | [One LR Doesn't Fit All: Heavy-Tail Guided Layerwise Learning Rates for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22297) | Layerwise Learning Rate turns Heavy-Tailed Self-Regularization from a diagnostic into a controller: it periodically fits each layer’s weight-correlation spectrum and assigns larger steps to weakly heavy-tailed layers and smaller steps to strongly self-regularized ones, then freezes the ratios after an early calibration window.
