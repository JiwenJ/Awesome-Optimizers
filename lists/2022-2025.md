## 2025

| Date | Paper Title | Summary |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 2025-12-18 | [Muon is Provably Faster with Momentum Variance Reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16598) | Muon-MVR imports STORM-style variance-reduced momentum into the Gluon linear-minimization-oracle framework, correcting momentum with same-sample gradient differences and extending the analysis to general norms and layerwise generalized smoothness. It improves the nonconvex rate from O(K^-1/4) to O(K^-1/3), and the star-convex rate from about O(K^-1/3) to O(K^-1/2); one 124M NanoGPT run improves loss from 3.832 to 3.682, but extra gradient-difference cost, transient spikes, and large-scale robustness remain unquantified. |
| 2025-12-15 | [Better LMO-based Momentum Methods with Second-Order Information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13227) | These LMO momentum methods add a Hessian–vector correction along the previous step—evaluated either at a random interpolation point or the new iterate—to exploit second-order information without forming the Hessian, unlike ordinary Polyak momentum. Under relaxed gradient smoothness (and Hessian smoothness for one variant) they achieve O(K^-1/3) nonconvex stationarity versus O(K^-1/4), while small MLP/LSTM experiments are mixed; no Muon-scale LLM or wall-clock study establishes whether the extra HVP pays off. |
| 2025-12-10 | [The Ky Fan Norms and Beyond: Dual Norms and Combinations for Matrix Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09678) | By deriving duals of Ky Fan k-norms, this paper shows their LMO is a rank-k sum of leading singular-vector pairs and builds the Fanion family, which connects rank-one Neon, low-rank Dion, and full-rank Muon; conic combinations further mix normalized or sign updates. Thick-restart Lanczos makes low-rank variants practical, but CIFAR-10 and GPT experiments mostly match rather than beat Muon, and native PyTorch support and theory explaining the empirical trade-off are absent. |
| 2025-12-05 | [Hyperparameter Transfer Enables Consistent Gains of Matrix-Preconditioned Optimizers Across Scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05620) | This work derives width/depth scaling rules for the learning rate and epsilon of Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon under μP, including blocking and grafting, and argues compute-optimal independent weight decay should scale inversely with width. FineWeb pretraining from 190M to 1.4B yields roughly 1.4× compute speedups over tuned AdamW, while incorrect scaling erases most of Muon’s gain; reported FLOP speedups exclude optimizer-transform overhead, span under one decade of width, and may not extrapolate to larger models. |
| 2025-12-04 | [Turbo-Muon: Almost-Orthogonal Pre-Conditioning for Fast Muon Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04632) [Code](https://github.com/thib-s/flash-newton-schulz) | Turbo-Muon replaces Frobenius initialization of Newton–Schulz with a cheap Gershgorin/AOL column preconditioner, reuses the initial Gram matrix, and removes one of five iterations while remaining a descent direction under an induced norm. On 4096×4096 matrices the core routine is 2.83× faster, and NanoGPT, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet runs save up to 3.6% end-to-end time at similar quality; theory assumes the exact polar factor even though deployment uses a finite approximation. |
| 2025-12-03 | [When do spectral gradient updates help in deep learning?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04299) | The paper predicts a spectral block update beats a Euclidean one when the gradient’s squared nuclear-to-Frobenius ratio exceeds the incoming activation’s stable rank, then proves low stable rank at Gaussian initialization and dimension-growing gradient nuclear rank over long windows in spiked random-feature models. Synthetic and NanoGPT diagnostics support the criterion and a SwiGLU counterexample violates it as predicted, but the analysis compares ideal one-step SpecGD with GD—not practical momentum Muon or AdamW—and large-transformer causal ablations remain open. |
| 2025-10-12 | [Preconditioned Norms: A Unified Framework for Steepest Descent, Quasi-Newton and Adaptive Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10777) [Code](https://github.com/brain-lab-research/LIB/tree/quasi_descent) | Preconditioned matrix norms unify steepest descent, Kronecker quasi-Newton, and elementwise adaptive methods by applying a base-norm LMO in a transformed gradient space, yielding necessary-and-sufficient affine/scale-invariance conditions and the spectral–adaptive hybrids MuAdam and MuAdam-SANIA. DistilBERT GLUE, Qwen2-7B LoRA, and small Shakespeare tests are competitive but inconsistent across tasks; the work gives invariance rather than convergence guarantees and does not test full LLM pretraining or quantify preconditioning overhead. |
| 2025-10-07 | [NorMuon: Making Muon more efficient and scalable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05491) [Code](https://github.com/zichongli5/NorMuon) | NorMuon keeps Muon’s orthogonalization but tracks one second-moment scalar per output neuron and row-normalizes the orthogonalized update, addressing Muon’s uneven neuron norms with negligible state; its FSDP2 implementation distributes full-matrix orthogonalizations and uses shard-local normalization. Across 124M–5.4B pretraining it reduces steps to AdamW’s loss by 21.74% at 1.1B and 13.91% at 5.4B, but increases communication volume by 33–50% and offers no convergence analysis. |
| 2025-10-06 | [DP-Adam-AC: Privacy-preserving Fine-Tuning of Localizable Language Models Using Adam Optimization with Adaptive Clipping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05288) | DP-Adam-AC augments DP-Adam with history-percentile adaptive clipping, clip-rate-driven learning-rate nudges, evaluation-only EMA, microbatch clipping, and a variable-sampling-rate Rényi privacy accountant, targeting short private fine-tuning of locally runnable models. One-epoch Qwen2.5-0.5B and BitNet-2B tests show the expected privacy–loss trade-off at ε≈1.75–12.57, but use only two synthetic 10,000-example datasets, measure mainly training loss, and omit competing private optimizers, leaving utility and generality weakly established. |
| 2025-10-04 | [Explore the Loss space with Hill-ADAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03613) | Hill-ADAM deterministically alternates Adam minimization and maximization whenever loss improvement falls below a threshold, resets moments at each switch, escapes runaway ascent with a loss ceiling, and returns the best visited state instead of settling at the first basin. It finds equal or lower minima on five hand-designed polynomials and a 12-case color-correction task, but has no global-convergence guarantee, is sensitive to switching/ceiling thresholds and learning rate, and is untested on standard high-dimensional learning benchmarks. |
| 2025-10-01 | [DeMuon: A Decentralized Muon for Matrix Optimization over Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01377) | DeMuon combines local Muon matrix-sign steps with gradient tracking and graph mixing so heterogeneous peers approach both stationarity and consensus without a central server; DeMuon-A adds multi-extrapolation under higher-order smoothness. Dimension-free analysis gives O(K^-1/4) stationarity for DeMuon and O(K^-p/(3p+1)) for the accelerated variant, while an eight-node 7.4M GPT experiment beats decentralized SGD baselines across three topologies; theory assumes exact matrix sign, and empirical scale and communication-cost evidence are limited. |
| 2025-09-29 | [Conda: Column-Normalized Adam for Training Large Language Models Faster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24218) | Conda periodically estimates an orthogonal subspace by SVD, projects Adam’s first and second moments into it, and applies column-wise variance normalization, retaining Adam adaptivity while avoiding Muon’s full singular-value flattening; lazy updates every 2,000 steps limit overhead. LLaMA 60M–1B and GPT-2 tests report up to 2.7× step-based and about 2.5× wall-clock convergence speed over AdamW plus better downstream results, but 13B and MoE scalability are untested and SVD adds state and compute. |
| 2025-09-19 | [On the Convergence of Muon and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15816) [Code](https://github.com/MaeChd/MUON-MVR) | Muon-MVR2 corrects momentum using same-minibatch gradients at the current and previous iterates, unlike cheaper one-batch MVR1, and with horizon-free schedules attains the optimal anytime O(T^-1/3) stochastic nonconvex rate versus O(T^-1/4) for standard/MVR1 Muon, plus PL guarantees. CIFAR-10 and 130M LLaMA/C4 tests favor MVR2 per step, but its second gradient roughly doubles wall time; theory assumes exact matrix signs and omits finite Newton–Schulz, practical schedules, and weight decay. |
| 2025-09-19 | [Towards Simple and Provable Parameter-Free Adaptive Gradient Methods](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dHxM51W120) | AdaGrad++ and Adam++ replace a tuned global learning rate with a simple trajectory-distance scale—maximum distance from initialization, normalized by dimension and, for Adam, time—while preserving the coordinatewise preconditioning of their namesakes. In convex stochastic optimization, AdaGrad++ retains O(T^-1/2) and Adam++ matches Adam’s rate; CIFAR-10 and GPT-2 experiments show modest gains, but nonconvex guarantees are absent and the experimentally used AdamW++ has no proof. |
| 2025-09-19 | [Towards Simple and Provable Parameter-Free Adaptive Gradient Methods](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dHxM51W120) | AdaGrad++ and Adam++ replace a tuned global learning rate with a simple trajectory-distance scale—maximum distance from initialization, normalized by dimension and, for Adam, time—while preserving the coordinatewise preconditioning of their namesakes. In convex stochastic optimization, AdaGrad++ retains O(T^-1/2) and Adam++ matches Adam’s rate; CIFAR-10 and GPT-2 experiments show modest gains, but nonconvex guarantees are absent and the experimentally used AdamW++ has no proof. |
| 2025-09-15 | [Low-rank Orthogonalization for Large-scale Matrix Optimization with Applications to Foundation Model Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11983) [Code](https://github.com/dengzhanwang/Low-rank-Muon) | Low-rank Muon Gaussian-sketches momentum, QR-factorizes the sketch, orthogonalizes only QᵀM, then lifts the result, replacing full Newton–Schulz while suppressing noise-sensitive tail singular directions. It proves near-optimal MSGD stationarity and optimal epsilon dependence under heavy-tailed noise, while GPT-2/LLaMA tests through 1B usually beat tuned Muon at ranks 100–200; end-to-end savings are modest, small models can regress, and rank selection or safeguarded error checks add overhead. |
| 2025-09-15 | [SRON: State-free LLM Training via Row-wise Gradient Normalization](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BtQLBWr6zI) | SRON rescales each two-dimensional gradient row by its current RMS before an SGD step, targeting attention-induced row-scale disparities without momentum, SVD, or persistent state; unlike global normalization, it preserves matrix structure. Under bounded-gradient/variance assumptions it proves O(log T/√T) nonconvex stationarity, and LLaMA/GPT/Gemma tests through 7B report 90–100% less optimizer state and up to 67% less time; the best hybrid still uses Adam for nonmatrix parameters, while fully state-free SRON† loses accuracy. |
| 2025-09-03 | [Understanding and Improving Shampoo and SOAP via Kullback-Leibler Minimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03378) [Code](https://github.com/yorkerlin/KL-Methods) | KL-Shampoo jointly estimates both Kronecker covariance factors by minimizing Gaussian KL divergence, then uses infrequent QR bases and per-step EMA eigenvalue corrections, unlike Shampoo’s one-sided Frobenius-motivated factors or SOAP’s full Adam state. Across four language models up to 227M, plus a 450M check, 150-run searches show it beats Shampoo/SOAP without grafting at SOAP-like iteration cost; no optimizer convergence theorem is given, and evidence remains sub-billion-scale after unusually extensive tuning. |
| 2025-09-01 | [Learning a Zeroth-Order Optimizer for Fine-Tuning LLMs](https://openreview.net/forum?id=2Dn4yHYLQJ) | ZO Fine-tuner replaces MeZO’s fixed isotropic Gaussian sampling with tiny per-parameter-block networks that predict normalized perturbation variances from compressed model and loss statistics, meta-trained once along a first-order trajectory and then reused. A block-diagonal-Hessian argument gives a tighter one-step expected-loss bound, and four LLMs across seven datasets win 82.1% of loss comparisons at near-MeZO memory; the result is not a global convergence theorem, and diagonal variances ignore cross-parameter correlations. |
| 2025-07-15 | [AdaMuon: Adaptive Muon Optimizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11005) [Code](https://github.com/niuxiangqi/SoftClippingMuon) | AdaMuon sign-transforms first-moment matrices before Newton–Schulz, tracks an elementwise EMA of squared orthogonal directions, divides by that RMS, and rescales to Adam-like update magnitude, adding coordinate adaptivity absent from Muon. GPT-2 and Qwen2.5 pretraining experiments through 7B report up to 40% token-efficiency gains, while an O(log T/√T) bound assumes directional alignment; the full second-moment buffer forfeits Muon’s memory advantage and adds compute. |
| 2025-06-20 | [Memory-Efficient LLM Pretraining via Minimalist Optimizer Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16659) [Code](https://github.com/OptimAI-Lab/Minimalist_LLM_Pretraining) | SCALE normalizes every matrix-gradient column and applies first-moment momentum only to the output layer, retaining essentially SGD elsewhere; this bottom-up design seeks the minimum useful state rather than compressing Adam’s two moments. LLaMA/Qwen experiments from 60M to 7B match or beat Adam, Muon, GaLore, Fira, and APOLLO using 35–45% of Adam’s total memory, with theory supporting variance-targeted momentum; evidence stops at 7B/100B tokens and the 7B comparison is a single run. |
| 2025-06-08 | [A Stable Whitening Optimizer for Efficient Neural Network Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07254) [Code](https://github.com/kvfrans/splus) | SPlus replaces Shampoo’s inverse-square-root preconditioned gradient with a bounded instant-sign update in a historical eigenbasis, adds symmetric shape scaling for width-transferable learning rates, and averages fast iterates to suppress high-rate noise. Transformer language, vision, and diffusion tests match Adam in 44–58% of steps and 62–83% of wall time, but the empirically designed method lacks convergence theory, uses roughly 60% more memory than Adam for square layers, and still pays periodic eigendecompositions. |
| 2025-05-29 | [On the Convergence Analysis of Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23737) | This paper does not alter Muon; it analyzes exact matrix-sign updates under Frobenius-, spectral-, and nonuniform-smoothness models, showing any advantage over GD arises when blockwise Hessians are low rank or the trajectory-averaged curvature J is small. Quadratic, MNIST, MLP, and six-layer GPT-2 experiments track the predicted ratios, but the evidence is small-scale and relies on exact orthogonalization plus structural, star-convex, or bounded-third-derivative assumptions rather than production Muon. |
| 2025-05-27 | [PolarGrad: A Class of Matrix-Gradient Optimizers from a Unifying Preconditioning Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21799) | PolarGrad multiplies a gradient’s polar factor by its nuclear norm, restoring update magnitudes that vanish with the gradient, and replaces Muon’s tuned Newton–Schulz approximation with QDWH or ZOLO-PD. Under smooth PL objectives this yields linear convergence where constant-step matrix-sign descent reaches a floor; matrix problems and small Qwen/GPT-2 runs are favorable, but momentum/non-PL theory is left open, GPT-2 losses are often similar, and scalable distributed implementations remain future work. |
| 2025-05-22 | [The Polar Express: Optimal Matrix Sign Methods and Their Application to the Muon Algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16932) | Polar Express replaces Muon’s fixed Newton–Schulz polynomial with per-iteration odd polynomials chosen by a minimax problem over the current singular-value interval, plus safeguards for bfloat16. It proves worst-case-optimal, super-exponential polar-factor convergence and gives small but consistent GPT-2 gains over recent five-step alternatives from 1B to 10B tokens; benefits shrink with longer training, CIFAR results are inconclusive, and the method depends on a supplied lower singular-value bound. |
| 2025-05-19 | [Gluon: Making Muon & Scion Great Again! (Bridging Theory and Practice of LMO-based Optimizers for LLMs)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13416) | Gluon formalizes practical Muon/Scion as independent layer-wise linear-minimization-oracle steps with adaptive radii and introduces layer-wise (L0,L1)-smoothness, replacing prior global smoothness analyses that prescribe unusably small steps. Deterministic theory produces radii close to tuned values, stochastic convergence is also established, and 124M NanoGPT plus CIFAR-10 trajectories support the model; however, theory assumes exact LMOs and bounded variance, while adaptive-radius guarantees remain deterministic. |
| 2025-05-04 | [Practical Efficiency of Muon for Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02222) | Rather than comparing optimizers at fixed resources, this study maps compute-time Pareto frontiers across model sizes, batch sizes, and TPU counts. Muon needs about 10-15% fewer tokens than AdamW at matched training loss, with the advantage remaining beyond the critical batch size; a 3.7B model trained for 160B tokens combines Muon with maximal-update parameterization and telescoping hyperparameter transfer. The proposed tuning strategy reduces search complexity to O(C log N), but evaluation uses fresh-batch training loss as a generalization proxy, relies on an in-house JAX implementation, and provides no downstream evaluation outside language pretraining. |
| 2025-04-22 | [Muon Optimizer Accelerates Grokking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16041) | Across small modular-arithmetic and parity tasks with softmax, stablemax, and sparsemax attention, this study reports that Muon reaches stable 95% validation accuracy earlier than AdamW: pooled mean grokking epochs are 102.89 versus 153.09, with a reported p-value of 6.33e-8. It establishes an early empirical link between orthogonalized updates and grokking but does not isolate Newton-Schulz from update scaling, pool task effects with limited per-task detail, and remains confined to small algorithmic transformers; later work provides the missing mechanism ablation. |
| 2025-04-07 | [Dion: Distributed Orthonormalized Updates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05295) [Code](https://github.com/microsoft/dion) | Dion replaces Muon’s full-matrix Newton–Schulz pass with amortized power iteration on a slowly changing momentum buffer; sharding-aware low-rank factors, rank fraction, and error feedback compress both computation and communication. GPT-style models through 3B retain Muon-like convergence and hyperparameter transfer, with larger models tolerating lower rank, but extreme-scale timing is simulated rather than trained, results cover only language models, and low ranks degrade quality unless error feedback and rank are carefully chosen. |
| 2025-02-24 | [COSMOS: A Hybrid Adaptive Optimizer for Memory-Efficient Training of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17410) [Code](https://github.com/lliu606/COSMOS) | COSMOS tracks only the leading eigenspace of the gradient second moment, applies SOAP-style coordinate adaptation there, and uses a Muon matrix-sign update on the orthogonal residual, unlike either optimizer alone. LLaMA/GPT-2 tests through 1B improve perplexity with roughly 13d² state versus SOAP’s 66d² and near-Muon wall time, but there is no convergence theory, rank and mixture weights still need choices, and 1B comparisons omit SOAP because of resource limits. |
| 2025-02-24 | [Muon is Scalable for LLM Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16982) [Code](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Moonlight) | This report makes Muon scalable by adding decoupled weight decay, shape-dependent scaling that fixes update RMS and matches AdamW’s learning-rate convention, plus a ZeRO-1-style sharded momentum implementation that gathers only the matrix being orthogonalized. Scaling-law fits estimate 52% of AdamW’s FLOPs, and Moonlight trains a 3B-active/16B-total MoE for 5.7T tokens; evidence centers on one proprietary pipeline, distributed Muon adds communication, and pretrain/finetune optimizer mismatch remains unresolved. |
| 2025-02-11 | [Training Deep Learning Models with Norm-Constrained LMOs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07529) [Code](https://github.com/LIONS-EPFL/scion) | Scion chooses architecture-aware norm balls and applies layerwise linear minimization oracles—sign for vector-like groups and spectral/polar updates for matrices—optionally constraining weights, rather than learning Adam-style geometry online. Its uSCG/SCG theory gives order-optimal O(n^-1/4) stochastic nonconvex stationarity without knowing smoothness, while NanoGPT through 3B and vision tests show learning-rate transfer, large-batch robustness, and one-weight/one-gradient memory; guarantees still assume smoothness and bounded variance, and large-scale evidence is limited to a single 3B run. |

## 2024

| Date | Paper Title | Summary |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 2024-12-08 | [Muon: An optimizer for hidden layers in neural networks](https://kellerjordan.github.io/posts/muon/) [Code](https://github.com/KellerJordan/Muon) | Muon orthogonalizes Nesterov momentum for each two-dimensional weight with five GPU-friendly Newton–Schulz steps, approximating UVᵀ and therefore spectral-norm steepest descent; embeddings, heads, and vectors stay on AdamW. The blog reports a 35% NanoGPT speedrun gain and favorable 1.5B runs with sub-1% estimated FLOP overhead, but offers no convergence proof and explicitly leaves 20B-plus/trillion-token scaling, distributed execution, finetuning, and reinforcement learning untested. |
| 2024-12-02 | [PROFIT: A Specialized Optimizer for Deep Fine Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01930) | PROFIT takes temporary reference-optimizer steps from the current converged model, treats their displacement as an implicit old-task direction, projects a new-task gradient away from it when they conflict, restores the weights, then applies any main optimizer—without old data or replay. Theory gives local old-loss protection and stable-point properties, and vision/VLM/motion tests reduce forgetting, but no full convergence proof is supplied; each update needs extra batches and roughly 25% more memory in the vanilla implementation. |
| 2024-11-25 | [Cautious Optimizers: Improving Training with One Line of Code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16085) [Code](https://github.com/kyleliang919/C-Optim) | Cautious optimizers mask coordinates where a momentum-based proposal disagrees in sign with the current gradient, then renormalize surviving coordinates, adding one line to AdamW, Lion, or MARS without retuning. Lyapunov/Hamiltonian analysis preserves base-optimizer descent guarantees, and LLM plus Mini-ImageNet experiments improve stability, but it does not prove a strictly faster global rate; after exhaustive tuning, 130M–1.2B perplexity gains are only 0.1–1%, and broader RL/continual-learning tests remain future work. |
| 2024-11-15 | [MARS: Unleashing the Power of Variance Reduction for Training Large Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10438) [Code](https://github.com/AGI-Arena/MARS) | MARS injects a scaled STORM correction—the same-sample gradient difference between current and previous iterates—into AdamW, Lion, or Shampoo, clips the corrected estimator, and builds preconditioners from it. Under smoothness, bounded variance, and positive-definite preconditioners it proves O(log T/T^(1/3)) convergence, while GPT-2 tests through 770M improve token and wall-clock efficiency; exact MARS needs a second gradient, so headline experiments use an approximation without the same guarantee, and evidence is confined largely to GPT-2. |
| 2024-11-11 | [Lean and Mean Adaptive Optimization via Subset-Norm and Subspace-Momentum with Convergence Guarantees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07120) [Code](https://github.com/timmytonga/sn-sm) | Subset-Norm shares AdaGrad-style scale estimates across carefully chosen coordinate groups, reducing adaptive-state memory from O(d) to O(sqrt(d)), while Subspace-Momentum stores momentum only in a low-dimensional subspace and applies SGD in its complement. Both receive high-probability nonconvex guarantees; combined on LLaMA-1B, they reached Adam's perplexity in 6.8B rather than 13.1B tokens while cutting optimizer-state memory by over 80% with little extra tuning. |
| 2024-11-05 | [ADOPT: Modified Adam Can Converge with Any $β_2$ with the Optimal Rate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02853) [Code](https://github.com/iShohei220/adopt) | ADOPT breaks the harmful dependence between Adam's current gradient and current variance estimate by normalizing with the previous second moment and applying normalization before the momentum update. These small ordering changes yield the optimal O(1/sqrt(T)) nonconvex rate for any beta2 without uniformly bounded gradient noise, unlike many Adam repairs; extensive image, generative, language, and reinforcement-learning experiments also outperformed Adam-family baselines. |
| 2024-10-25 | [COAT: Compressing Optimizer states and Activation for Memory-Efficient FP8 Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19313) [Code](https://github.com/NVlabs/COAT) | COAT extends FP8 beyond linear-layer arithmetic to the two major remaining memory consumers: optimizer states and saved activations. Dynamic Range Expansion reshapes state distributions to use FP8's range accurately, while mixed-granularity activation quantization chooses per-tensor or per-group scaling by operator sensitivity; LLM/VLM training retained near-lossless quality while using 1.54x less end-to-end memory and running 1.43x faster than BF16, enabling larger batches or fewer GPUs. |
| 2024-10-21 | [LDAdam: Adaptive Optimization from Low-Dimensional Gradient Statistics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16103) [Code](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/LDAdam) | LDAdam performs Adam updates in rotating low-rank subspaces while still exploring the full parameter space, introducing projection-aware moment transport so statistics remain meaningful when the basis changes. Block power iteration selects each subspace, and generalized error feedback corrects compression of both gradients and optimizer states; convergence follows under standard assumptions, while language-model pretraining and fine-tuning matched full Adam closely at a fraction of its state memory. |
| 2024-09-17 | [SOAP: Improving and Stabilizing Shampoo using Adam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11321) [Code](https://github.com/nikhilvyas/SOAP) | SOAP proves that half-power Shampoo is Adafactor in Shampoo's eigenbasis, then replaces Adafactor there with Adam and continuously updates diagonal moments between infrequent basis refreshes. This prevents the degradation caused by simply making Shampoo decompositions stale and leaves only refresh frequency beyond Adam's hyperparameters; on 360M/660M language models, SOAP cut iterations by over 40% and wall time by over 35% versus AdamW, improving both metrics about 20% over Shampoo. |
| 2024-09-05 | [The AdEMAMix Optimizer: Better, Faster, Older](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03137) [Code](https://github.com/apple/ml-ademamix) | AdEMAMix challenges single-timescale momentum by mixing a fast EMA for recent gradients with a very slow EMA that preserves information for tens of thousands of steps, then schedules the older component into Adam's update. The added long memory both accelerates optimization and reduces forgetting: a 1.3B model trained on 101B tokens matched an AdamW model trained on 197B, showing that old gradients remain useful far longer than conventional momentum assumes. |
| 2024-06-24 | [Adam-mini: Use Fewer Learning Rates To Gain More](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16793) [Code](https://github.com/zyushun/Adam-mini) | Adam-mini uses observed block structure in neural-network Hessians to replace Adam's per-parameter second moments with one adaptive rate per structurally chosen block while retaining first momentum. The analysis suggests at least 99.9% of Adam's distinct rates are redundant; across 39M–13B models, pretraining, SFT, and RLHF matched or beat AdamW with roughly 50% less optimizer memory, and Llama-2-7B throughput rose 49.6% on two A800 GPUs, saving 33% wall time. |
| 2024-05-24 | [MicroAdam: Accurate Adaptive Optimization with Low Space Overhead and Provable Convergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15593) [Code](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/MicroAdam) | MicroAdam sparsifies or compresses gradients before they enter Adam's moments, then controls the lost information with error feedback whose correction buffer is itself compressed—avoiding the full-size residual that would erase the memory gain. Its analysis shifts compression effects into higher-order terms and matches AMSGrad's asymptotic guarantees for smooth nonconvex and PL objectives; GPU experiments on BERT- and LLaMA-scale models remained competitive with uncompressed Adam in convergence and runtime while using less memory. |
| 2024-05-24 | [The Road Less Scheduled](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15682) [Code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/schedule_free) | Schedule-Free optimization derives a direct equivalence between learning-rate decay and iterate averaging, then evaluates gradients at an interpolation of running and averaged iterates so a constant-rate momentum optimizer can obtain scheduled-like behavior without knowing the stopping horizon. It adds no hyperparameters to standard momentum methods, works from convex problems through large-scale deep learning, and Schedule-Free AdamW won the self-tuning track of the 2024 MLCommons AlgoPerf challenge. |
| 2024-05-21 | [FAdam: Adam is a natural gradient optimizer using diagonal empirical Fisher information](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12807) [Code](https://github.com/lessw2020/fadam_pytorch) | FAdam derives Adam as an approximate natural-gradient method using the square root of a diagonal empirical Fisher metric, explicitly cataloguing the diagonal, empirical, and root approximations and warning that empirical Fisher is best justified for discrete log-likelihood losses. That geometry motivates corrected momentum and bias handling, adaptive epsilon, clipping, and metric-aware decay; the resulting optimizer improved LLM, VQ-VAE, and speech-recognition training, including reported state-of-the-art ASR results. |
