### October 2026

| Date | Paper Title | Relation |

| 2026-10-15 | [Does On-Policy Distillation Need to Complete Every Rollout?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21337) | STOPD randomly truncates student rollouts and uses blockwise inverse-probability weighting to preserve full-rollout gradient sums in expectation. Its Muon-specific analysis propagates gradient discrepancies through Nesterov momentum, stabilized Frobenius normalization, and finite polynomial orthogonalization using spectral divided differences, yielding a conditional one-step update-error bound. Dedicated Muon experiments with a 1.7B student and 4B teacher save 37% generated tokens and report 1.9x full-step speedup, with similar mathematical-task accuracy to complete-rollout distillation. These savings come from rollout estimation, not a faster Muon rule. Nonlinear optimizer updates are not themselves unbiased, and the bounds compare a shared previous state rather than guaranteeing identical long-run trajectories. |
| 2026-10-15 | [The Ephemeral Cost of Switching Optimizers Across LLM Training Stages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22650) | Separates optimizer mismatch from each stage's optimizer effect by varying probabilities of AdamW versus Muon updates during pretraining, continued pretraining, and supervised fine-tuning. Controlled 1.2B Llama-3 experiments use approximately 24B or 193B pretraining tokens and three SFT seeds per setting. Matching optimizers reduces forgetting, but downstream benchmark interactions are small; continued-pretraining mismatch largely disappears after learning-rate tuning. Muon is competitive on AdamW-pretrained Llama/Qwen models up to 14B, although most open-model gaps are small relative to run variability. The study concerns task-general SFT and specific training recipes, not universal task-specific fine-tuning superiority or a newly proposed optimizer. |
| 2026-10-15 | [When Does Muon Actually Win? A Paired Data-, Time-, and Tuning-Budget Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21555) | Benchmarks reference single-device Muon against AdamW under matched data, measured training time, and 23-candidate development budgets, followed by five paired seeds and frozen test evaluation. On 17.7M/33.6M language models and 2.70M vision transformers, Muon improves every equal-data validation-loss AUC and all twenty test-loss pairs. Time-normalized AUC favors Muon on vision but AdamW on language; the complete Muon call, including auxiliary AdamW, consumes 56.1-69.6% of training time. Configurations are selected for data AUC, not independently time-tuned. Single-A100 reference kernels and small models limit transfer to optimized distributed training; this is benchmark evidence, not a new optimizer. |
| 2026-10-15 | [When Is Muon Optimal? Radial Fragility and Minimax Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21549) | Tests whether a curvature-surrogate explanation of Muon's polar direction survives replacing a fixed input radius with a random radial law. Exact admissibility becomes a translated spherical-zonoid condition, with nonscalar freedom arising only from radial atoms aligned with curvature kinks. Under atomless radii, exact scaled-polar optimality requires an already scaled-isometric full-column-rank gradient. Replacing directional averaging by worst-case directional laws restores a polar-ray minimizer and reduces scale selection to a scalar convex problem. These are exact one-step surrogate results, not optimization-error estimates, practical finite-Newton-Schulz guarantees, or training-dynamics experiments; concentrated atomless inputs may still make polar updates approximately optimal. |
| 2026-10-14 | [From Spectral to Frobenius: When Muon Loses Its Spectral Implicit Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20112) | Shows that fixed finite Newton-Schulz orthogonalization with positive stabilization can change Muon's asymptotic implicit bias from spectral to Frobenius max-margin in separable linear classification, including EMA and Nesterov-style momentum. The proof represents practical updates as gradient descent plus summable perturbations. Removing stabilization alone can produce neither max-margin direction. Logarithmically increasing cubic NS iterations recovers spectral bias under the stated schedules; the original quintic requires a special randomized, gradient-dependent stabilization scheme, and fixed-stabilization quintic recovery remains open. Synthetic experiments illustrate these regimes. This is not evidence that increasing Polar Express steps improves LLM training, nor a universal adaptive-NS prescription. |
| 2026-10-14 | [Long-Horizon RL Needs Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20256) | Studies Muon within single-rollout asynchronous reinforcement learning for repository-level issue resolution, combining token-averaged policy loss, truncated importance sampling, and auxiliary length and tool-error penalties. Experiments use R2E-Gym with MiniCPM5-2B and Qwen3.5-4B, comparing Adam and Muon SPO baselines and ablations of the broader SPO++ recipe. The full configuration achieves higher late-training patch-test success in the selected runs. However, headline comparisons jointly alter several recipe components, each run collects its own trajectories, and summaries use the longest available runs or last observations when complete windows are unavailable. This work-in-progress reports training success, not a new optimizer or a general held-out SWE benchmark guarantee. |
| 2026-10-13 | [A Refresh Interval Does Not Fully Specify a Refresh Schedule: Learning-Rate-Dependent Placement in Kronecker-Preconditioned Language Model Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18695) | This study varies the placement of a fixed number of curvature refreshes in Mousse, a curvature-augmented Muon optimizer, while preserving eigendecomposition cost. Across short-horizon GPT-style models from 64M to 929M on six corpora, earlier and later placements exchange ranking as the learning rate changes; an equal-budget uniform schedule usually beats both diagnostic extremes. The reversal persists with published optimizer internals and denser refresh budgets. A spectral-width staleness score misses it, motivating full schedule reporting and learning-rate sweeps. This is an empirical schedule-comparison study, not a newly established adaptive controller or evidence that one placement universally improves long-run training. |
| 2026-10-13 | [How Inner Optimizers Shape Replica Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19064) | Analyzes how nonlinear inner optimizers affect DiLoCo replica scaling through pseudogradient covariance, transformation-before-averaging mismatch, and second-moment estimation at smaller local batches. Whitening-based SOAP and SPlus scale better than Muon, Shampoo, and AdamW, and scaling second-moment memory reduces an Adam mean-mismatch bound. On 512M LLaMA with 96 replicas and 30 local steps, validation losses are SOAP 2.676, SPlus 2.663, Muon 2.787, and AdamW 2.958; their own data-parallel baselines differ. Experiments span 178M/512M models and 8-96 replicas with optimizer-specific tuning. The results concern communication-efficient local training, not a universal ranking for conventional synchronized pretraining. |
| 2026-10-13 | [Taming Softmax at the Limits of Muon: A Unified Approach through Hilbert Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17588) | Explains why controlling a linear layer's RMS output movement, as in Muon's derivation, can inadequately control the probability distribution after softmax. Hilbert distance instead equals the variation seminorm of logit perturbations and bounds cross-entropy changes and probability redistribution. For LM heads, the induced row-diameter constraint has an expensive exact dual; a common-enclosing-ball approximation recovers RowNorm. For attention, the first-order QK constraint recovers the problem underlying Compositional Muon. Centering alone leaves standard Muon unchanged. This is a unifying theoretical framework, with no new training-speed or model-quality experiments; Hilbert constraints may overrestrict harmless relative changes to very-low-probability outcomes. |
| 2026-10-13 | [Which Optimizer Leaks Most? Optimizer Implicit Bias as a Training-Data Privacy Controller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18294) | Connects checkpoint training-data reconstruction to optimizer-dependent KKT conditions and the continuous weight information retained by their implicit biases. Spectral Muon and Euclidean SGD conditions support reconstruction, whereas idealized Adam's infinity-norm bias can make the evaluated stationarity system locally underdetermined. On 500-example binary CIFAR-10, no-prior SSIM@30 is 0.5508 for Muon, 0.2287 for SGD, and 0.1379 for Adam. Experiments use small fully connected MNIST/CIFAR models; Muon only updates hidden matrices, and training-budget/architecture effects alter its advantage. Adam's resistance applies to this attack family and idealized regime, not differential privacy or a guarantee against other checkpoint attacks. |
| 2026-10-12 | [From Parallel to Sequential: Small-Batch Noise Restores Simplicity Bias of Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16868) | Shows that stochastic noise can qualitatively change spectral descent: full-batch Muon learns singular modes in parallel, while noise smooths the sign operator and restores gradient-dependent, sequential feature learning, especially at greater depth. Deep-linear expected-flow analysis derives depth-dependent implicit regularization under structured noise assumptions; symmetric heavy-tailed noise can retain the smoothing mechanism. Matrix reconstruction, DeiT, and 162M LLaMA experiments associate smaller batches with lower effective rank. At fixed learning rate, LLaMA token accuracy falls from 40.79% at batch 256 to 40.18% at 1024, but linear learning-rate scaling restores 40.78%. Thus rank trends do not imply that smaller batches universally improve quality. |
| 2026-10-10 | [Non-Euclidean Gradient Descent Has Many Edges of Stability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15223) | This paper analyzes fixed-step non-Euclidean descent on quadratic objectives through radial and projective dynamics. Unlike Hilbertian geometry's sharp almost-everywhere stability threshold, different attracting directions can produce coexisting convergent and divergent basins. A Muon-motivated spectral-norm appendix proves a local collapse of direction-selection and radial-stability thresholds at full-rank critical matrices. Crucially, the authors distinguish their duality-map iteration from Muon's normalized polar update, so these results are not a direct stability theorem for practical momentum Muon or finite Newton–Schulz implementations. Smoothness, nondegeneracy, full-rank locality, and simplified quadratic dynamics limit transfer; examples illustrate geometry rather than establish an LLM learning-rate prescription. |
| 2026-10-09 | [On the Depth Transfer of Neural Optimizers via L-Smoothness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14344) | Derives depth-uniform Lipschitz and smoothness bounds for residual networks with inverse-depth branch scaling, fixed width, bounded parameter domains, and uniform branch regularity, using arbitrary operator-norm geometries relevant to Muon, MOGA, and AdamW. These bounds remove an additional depth-dependent curvature penalty but do not alone guarantee an identical optimal learning rate. GPT-2 sweeps at width 256 cover 2-128 layers and 315M tokens per run; Muon/MOGA share nearly the same best tested rates. CIFAR-100 ResNets provide additional support. Validation-loss transfer is less consistent than training-loss transfer, and larger-model or joint width-depth guarantees are not established by these experiments. |
| 2026-10-08 | [From Geometry to Generalization: Why Row Normalization Can Beat Adam and Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11309) | Connects optimizer-selected max-margin geometry to population accuracy in high-dimensional multiclass Gaussian classification. Row-normalized updates better preserve class-mean decision directions than exact-SVD Muon or coordinate-wise Adam under stated isotropic and independently oriented anisotropic models; coordinate-aligned covariances can reverse the Adam comparison. In five-seed frozen-backbone LLaMA-60M head retraining, RowSGDM reaches perplexity 28.6466 versus Muon's 28.7027 and Adam's 28.7199, with a similar 130M ordering. This analyzes existing update families rather than introducing a new optimizer. Guarantees use stylized linear readouts, directional convergence assumptions and zero-stabilizer Adam; random-reshuffling Adam is handled through a proxy, not a general practical-Adam theorem. |
| 2026-10-06 | [The Best Optimizer Depends on Batch Size](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08975) | Tests hundreds of Muon hyperparameter-scaling rules and independently retunes optimizers across batch sizes. No single rule transfers across language modeling and CIFAR-5M; NanoGPT's roughly 1.7B-token benchmark changes winner from SOAP at 128K-token batches to Shampoo at 2M. A noisy-quadratic analysis links this reversal to curvature/noise-dependent preconditioning bias-variance tradeoffs. In checkpoint branching, keeping small-batch updates in the sharpest 0.001% of directions removes 35-59.5% of the local large-batch loss penalty. This costly fixed-subspace diagnostic is not a ready-to-use optimizer, does not establish universal rankings, and omits noise anisotropy when selecting directions. No official implementation was linked. |
| 2026-10-05 | [On the Comparison of Optimizers for Imbalanced Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06421) | This study compares continuous-time Euclidean, sign, spectral, and Newton geometries when minority structure is hidden inside the aggregate loss. Explicit minority-awareness regions and entry-time bounds show milder minority-amplitude dependence for normalized or spectral descent, without proving universal earlier entry on each instance. Practical tests include Muon and AdamW against SGD on CIFAR-10 and SmallGPT2/WikiText-103. Reported CIFAR minority accuracies average early iterations, not final accuracy, and classification uses zero momentum across three seeds. The theory idealizes small-step dynamics rather than finite Newton-Schulz Muon; language-model rare-token improvements can trade against majority loss. It supplies optimizer-geometry evidence, not a new optimizer. |
| 2026-10-03 | [Optimizer Geometry Sets the Pace: Spectral Learning Dynamics in Matrix Factorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04249) | Derives singular-mode birth, saturation and decay laws in deep matrix factorization under explicit alignment/balance conditions. Ideal momentum-free Muon removes the divergent small-initialization birth barrier and eliminates unsupported aligned modes in finite time, unlike GD's slow tails. Cumulative Shampoo retains an initial depth barrier before history-driven catch-up; K-FAC damping restores GD-like behavior below a spectral threshold. Deterministic double-precision deep-linear experiments validate the regimes with separately tuned integration steps and matched final losses. It proposes no new optimizer or practical performance ranking, and exact polar Muon, instantaneous Adam, cumulative Shampoo and a specified K-FAC block model differ from standard stochastic implementations. |
| 2026-10-02 | [Muon Learns Facts Better: Understanding the Role of Spectral Orthogonalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02798) | Analyzes population gradient flow in a single-head linear attention factual-recall model with fixed orthonormal embeddings. Spectral gradient flow, an idealized Muon limit, reduces the subject-versus-relation learning-time ratio from approximately sqrt(S/R) to order one and accelerates component error decay. Both ordinary and spectral flows respect orthogonal embedding transformations, whereas the Sign-flow approximation to Adam can change or reverse feature-learning order. ODE simulations vary subject/relation counts from four to 1,024 and corroborate these laws. This is not evidence that production Muon always learns facts better: minibatch noise, finite Newton-Schulz, realistic embeddings, deep transformers and ordinary Adam momentum are outside the analyzed setting. |
| 2026-10-02 | [OptiSelect: How does the Optimizer Shape Data Curriculum?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03432) | OptiSelect analyzes how optimizer-induced candidate utility scores shape online data selection. Under Gaussian scoring assumptions, it relates attainable selection gain to score discriminability and studies sign collapse and radial invariance of the ideal polar derivative. Experiments on 124M and 720M language models find AdamW's diagonal-adaptive scoring strongest even when Muon performs optimization. Muon's selection benefit varies with scale rather than disappearing universally. The theoretical polar-tangent map is not the implemented right-preconditioned proxy, which retains radial components, nor the exact finite Newton-Schulz update. Main gains concern applied-token efficiency, not compute savings: oversampled candidates and scoring work must also be counted. |
| 2026-10-02 | [Training-Loss Guarantees for Muon with Finite-Step Newton-Schulz Orthogonalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03306) | This analysis proves training-loss hitting guarantees for momentum Muon with five tuned Newton–Schulz iterations in sufficiently wide two-layer ReLU networks with fixed random output weights. It transfers update alignment near initialization to current-gradient descent using kernel stability, without exact polar orthogonalization. The target-dependent learning rate scales with one minus momentum, and the iteration bound worsens as momentum approaches one; it does not prove acceleration. Thirty teacher-student runs across six widths support alignment and stability diagnostics. The sufficient theoretical width is astronomically larger than tested widths, and full-batch lazy-training assumptions do not establish convergence or benefits for practical LLM pretraining. |
| 2026-10-01 | [Muon meets Tamed Langevin: Momentum Preconditioning beyond Convex and gradient-Lipschitz Potentials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02158) | Builds a kinetic Langevin sampler from a nonquadratic spectral momentum energy, adding state-dependent noise and a divergence correction so the continuous process preserves the target Gibbs position marginal; its zero-temperature limit recovers an epsilon-regularized Muon-type update. It proves exponential continuous-time convergence in weighted total variation and uniform-in-time discrete momentum bounds for smooth semiconvex potentials with polynomial gradient/Hessian growth, convexity at infinity, and sufficiently small steps. This is substantive Muon-adjacent sampling theory rather than an empirically validated training optimizer: there are no benchmark experiments or linked code, and discrete moment stability alone does not imply an exact invariant distribution or global optimization convergence. |

| 2026-09-30 | [Convergence of Practical Muon with Finite Newton-Schulz Iterations and Nesterov Momentum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39595) | This analysis treats layer-wise finite-step Newton–Schulz Muon and Nesterov momentum jointly on a coupled nonconvex objective. Under gradient-dependent generalized smoothness and conditionally unbiased stochastic gradients with bounded layer-wise variance, it bounds the expected average Frobenius gradient norm by O(T^-1/4). Unlike exact-polar idealizations, the proof requires neither bounded stochastic gradients, symmetric noise, nor a positive lower bound on nonzero output singular values. Scalar-map bounds are verified for the practical five-step quintic and allow varying coefficients satisfying the same conditions. Vanishing stationarity uses coupled learning-rate and momentum schedules; the theorem is not a guarantee for arbitrary constant hyperparameters or an empirical optimizer superiority claim. |
| 2026-09-30 | [The Conflict Between Logic and Memory: Training Conditions for Optimizer-Dependent Rule Acquisition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00403) | This study compares optimizer-dependent acquisition of generalizing relations in shallow ReLU networks using nuisance-heavy composite tasks, parameter freezing, and a three-seed parity scan. Fixing nuisance-connected first-layer weights raises the hardest-stage accuracy from near chance to 70.56%, 68.47%, and 69.00% for momentum SGD, Adam, and Muon. On another task, the same intervention reduces Muon's rare-mode advantage over momentum SGD by 12.48 points. Post-training masking does not reproduce the benefit, distinguishing learning conditions from inference expression. Condition-specific validation selects learning rates and checkpoints, so results diagnose mechanisms rather than establish a universally superior optimizer or a new optimization rule. |
| 2026-09-30 | [The Row Normalization Puzzle in Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39114) | This analysis isolates how NorMuon's row normalization changes gradient alignment in operator-norm geometry. A rank-one construction yields a row-dimension-dependent deterministic lower bound, matched by an upper bound; the penalty persists with exact polar factors and fixed momentum, and approximate-polar stochastic bounds are also derived. Synthetic tests reproduce the slowdown, while pretraining at 286M, 1.38B, and 6.44B total parameters favors NorMuon in validation loss. The 1.38B comparison includes three seeds; CIFAR-10 separates accuracy from loss gains. The result is a worst-case/practice tension, not a new optimizer or evidence that normalization universally hurts training. |
| 2026-09-29 | [Making Analog Training Scale: Co-Designing Mapping, Optimizer, and Converters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36584) | An analog-training simulation study co-designing logical weight coordinates, optimizer-to-pulse conversion, residual accumulation, mapping, and converters rather than introducing a new standalone optimizer. It evaluates existing first-order and matrix optimizers, including Muon and Moonlight, and reports a 123.6M-parameter scaling exponent of -0.231 versus digital training's -0.238. A persistent loss gap remains, hardware results are simulated, and optimizer-specific pulse conversion complicates comparison. Relevant to deployment of matrix optimizers on analog hardware, but not evidence of a generally superior digital update rule. No paper-specific official implementation was located. |
| 2026-09-29 | [Optimizer-dependent training dynamics converge to the same one-third optimal data scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37745) | Studies online single-layer teacher–student softmax learning: optimizer-dependent radial and tangential exponents satisfy 2αr+αt=1, producing a common optimally tuned one-third data-scaling envelope across seven optimizers. Muon's fitted envelope exponent is 0.332±0.003, but its effective dynamics are inferred empirically rather than derived from the update rule; the result is a toy-model scaling analysis, not a deep-LLM optimizer or training recipe. |
| 2026-09-29 | [Second-Moment Stochastic Approximation Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36600) | Unifies Muon, ASGO, and Adam as approximations to optimal second-moment preconditioning under different gradient-noise structures, then separates nominal dynamics from estimator bias and variance. Under an aiming condition and bounded deviation it proves almost-sure convergence to a neighborhood, not generally to the exact solution. A 1.81B LLaMA-style model trained on 32 H100s illustrates alignment and hyperparameter effects, but the target is approximated by the final checkpoint; this analyzes existing methods rather than introducing a distinct optimizer. |
| 2026-09-29 | [Why Adaptive Optimizers Underestimate Rare Tokens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37535) | Analyzes softmax output-layer fixed points and mean-embedding conservation: Muon and Shampoo preserve the stated conservation property, while several adaptive/sign methods do not. Periodic-unigram RMSProp can systematically underestimate rare tokens, and increasing Adam's second-moment memory mitigates bias in small synthetic language models. Evidence is unigram and Markov-chain data with a width-64 residual MLP rather than real LLM pretraining, and no new optimizer or public repository is established. |
| 2026-09-28 | [Can Representation Learning Decouple from Loss Minimization? Polar Updates Have an Answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36240) | Shows that full-batch exact-polar updates can keep improving teacher-subspace alignment during long period-two, flat-loss trajectories; projected/refitted loss can be 5–11 times below the apparent plateau. Proofs cover constrained linear and mean-field ReLU settings, with shallow-network and residual controls. Momentum, minibatch noise, and approximate Newton–Schulz transforms alter or disrupt the studied dynamics, so this is not evidence that practical Muon should deliberately tolerate a prolonged pretraining-loss plateau. |
| 2026-09-28 | [Muon Sublates the Edge of Stability in LLM Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34915) | Derives a loss-neutral stochastic polar-update boundary 2ρ_b/η and distinguishes within-step conditional stability from temporal update alignment. In 130M and 1B language-model pretraining, Muon can track this edge without deterministic period-two reversal; larger batches strengthen only partial cancellation. The theory omits momentum and practical NS/auxiliary-optimizer details create numerical gaps. Official training and conditional-loss/alignment diagnostic code is public, with expensive reference-loss and exact-SVD probes. |
| 2026-09-27 | [Convergence of Practical Muon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33152) | Analyzes practical quintic Newton–Schulz Muon with decoupled weight decay as right-preconditioned descent with a dynamic weighted-L2 term, obtaining an expected Frobenius-gradient O(T^-1/4) nonconvex rate. Its stated dimension dependence improves on a compared AdamW bound, but norm choices, smoothness/variance assumptions, and horizon-tuned hyperparameters constrain the comparison. ResNet-32 and 124M GPT-2 experiments illustrate theory rather than establishing a new optimizer or a universal NS-iteration prescription. |
| 2026-09-26 | [Muon Under Gradient Noise and the Limits of Orthogonalization Near Optima](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32861) | Near a quadratic optimum under Gaussian gradient noise, the mean polar response becomes locally linear while a residual adds stationary covariance; momentum reduces but does not remove it. Frozen Tiny Shakespeare gradient-pool diagnostics find covariance ratios around 1.42–1.45 without momentum and 1.13–1.14 with Nesterov 0.95, with similar exact-polar and NS-5 results. No optimizer-switch training experiment, practical switch detector, or learning-rate-matching rule is provided, so this is local noise analysis rather than a validated late-training replacement. |
| 2026-09-23 | [NS-ATTENTION: Newton-Schulz Transformations of Attention Outputs in Vision Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27735) | Applies one Muon-inspired Newton–Schulz polynomial step to each attention head's feature-by-token output while preserving its Frobenius norm; this is an activation transform rather than an optimizer. Across ViT/Swin on CIFAR-10/100 it gains 0.25–0.83 points and wins all 12 paired seeds, but forward latency rises 41–104% and evaluation uses only three seeds and two small datasets. The September-25 v3 now links the official NS-Attention implementation, configurations, sanity tests, and raw classification/spectral/timing results, correcting the initial release's lack of code. |
| 2026-09-21 | [Muon Can Outperform Dedicated Continual Learning Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24678) | Applies unchanged Muon to incremental LoRA rather than proposing an optimizer: IncLoRA overall accuracy rises from 75.83±4.33 with AdamW to 81.24±2.80, and TRACE gains 3.42–7.62 points across methods. The study attributes the result to broader update spectra, but ranking among dedicated continual-learning methods remains unresolved, TRACE uses only three seeds and one order, penalty strengths are not retuned, and no official code is linked. |
| 2026-09-19 | [Optimizers for Diffusion Models: A Controlled Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23055) | Benchmarks seven existing optimizers across four diffusion formulations with matched tuning and three-seed retraining, rather than proposing an optimizer. Muon reaches LM1B perplexity 61.6 versus AdamW's 68.2 and QM9 2.069 versus 2.072, but does not beat tuned AdamW on text8 or CelebA-64. Models range from 16.6M to 137M; LM1B uses only 30k steps versus the reference 1M and remains unconverged, rankings vary by formulation, and the claimed official code repository currently returns 404. |
| 2026-09-18 | [HyperParallel-FSDP: Topology-Aware Fully Sharded Training with Layout-Driven Muon on Ascend SuperPods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21594) | HyperParallel-FSDP co-designs above-autograd distributed tensors, topology-aware fully sharded training, and layout-driven Muon execution. Muon communication groups follow sharding semantics, redundant orthogonalization is removed across shard and replica domains, and same-shaped matrices share batched Newton–Schulz work. On sixteen Ascend NPUs, profiler step time improves 5.4–16.0% over the evaluated distributed-Muon integrations. Separate FSDP comparisons report 29.7% and 25.5% lower step time, while a 505B MoE reaches 421k tokens/s at 768 ranks. Those system gains belong to different configurations; the largest scale has no matched baseline. This is distributed implementation research, not a changed Muon optimization geometry. |
| 2026-09-17 | [Design of the IBM Granite 5.0 TurboCTC ASR Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20104) | Granite 5.0 TurboCTC routes two-dimensional operator weights to MuonP and the remaining parameters to AdamW; its controlled optimizer comparison reports average WER 5.99 for AdamW, 5.93 for Muon, and 5.83 for MuonP. The result comes from one 470M-parameter, roughly 60,000-hour ASR recipe, while the final model also changes distillation and robustness training, so the optimizer does not explain the full reported speed-accuracy frontier. |
| 2026-09-15 | [Fenchel-Young Duality Gaps: Certified Early Stopping for Regularized Inverse Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17629) | This work interprets Muon-style singular-value thresholding as a nuclear-norm proximal step and uses Fenchel-Young duality gaps to provide oracle-free stopping certificates, including a 10,000-step ill-conditioned matrix-sensing demonstration where the certificate tracks a true optimality-gap upper bound. The guarantee applies to a convex proximal surrogate rather than nonconvex deep-network training and introduces no new optimizer. |
| 2026-09-13 | [Depth and Scale in the Sub-150M Regime: JugnuLM-53M vs JugnuLM-110M](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14715) | JugnuLM routes 81.4M attention and MLP parameters through Muon and 28.3M remaining parameters through AdamW. At matched data, budget, and seed, adding Muon to the value-residual model raises ARC-Easy from 54.67 to 56.10 and improves early validation perplexity, but lowers BLiMP from 81.10 to 80.78 and leaves only about a 1% perplexity advantage by 8.4B tokens. The evidence is a single-seed 110M-model ablation coupled to a value-residual architecture. |
| 2026-09-11 | [ZGCM-1: A Fully Open and Extremely Efficient Foundation Model for Math and Agentic Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13356) | ZGCM-1 pretrains a 7B model on 4.19T tokens with hybrid FP8 Muon for matrices and Adam for scalar parameters, including logical splitting of fused QKV operators. The report estimates roughly 1.8x better step-to-loss efficiency from Muon and about 4.2x overall pipeline gain, but the latter combines FP8, architecture, normalization, systems, and optimizer changes rather than a controlled Muon ablation. |
| 2026-09-03 | [LLaDA-Image: Building Strong Image Generators with Fully Open Training Recipes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03796) | LLaDA-Image trains a 6B diffusion Transformer from scratch with parameter-free RMSNorm and Muon across every image-generation stage, covering 220M cumulative samples; the published recipe uses learning rates from 4e-4 down to 5e-6, zero weight decay, and gradient clipping at 1.0. The released model reports 53.53 and 53.38 on the English and Chinese Qwen-Image-Bench tracks and includes weights, code, and detailed recipes, but Muon is not ablated against another optimizer, so its contribution cannot be separated from the data curriculum, architecture, normalization, and distillation stack. |
